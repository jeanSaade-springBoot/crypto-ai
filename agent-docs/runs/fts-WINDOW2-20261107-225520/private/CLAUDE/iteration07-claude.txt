# CLAUDE: FTS WINDOW2, Iteration 7 (self-challenge)

## Summary

I checked my iteration-6 verdicts against the I6-001 to I6-005 results. Three of them need correcting, one is reframed, and two hold up:

1. **Corrected: "Higher Entry Quality goes with more losses."** This was mostly a volatility mix effect. Once entries are grouped by ATR (average true range, a volatility measure), the gradient disappears in the low and high bands. ATR band explains outcomes better than Entry Quality does. Nearly all of the population's net loss (−4.13 of −4.17 USDT) is in the band where 1m ATR is 0.25% or more.
2. **Withdrawn: "Require a current 1m BUY at execution."** Across the population, entries made while the 1m signal was WATCH did slightly *better* than entries made on a 1m BUY.
3. **Softened: "1m STRONG labels flip within a minute."** Only 30 of 920 1m BUYs (3.3%) were followed by a bearish 1m one minute later. The flips I saw in the frozen trades went from bearish to BUY. That is a pattern in these trades, not a general finding.
4. **Reframed: the frozen sample is unrepresentative.** Five of the six frozen trades are low-volatility entries. Low-volatility entries as a group are close to breakeven (−0.12 USDT over 69 positions). Their losses are noise in a bucket that does not drive losses, not the system's main way of losing money.
5. **Confirmed: the noise-entry picture for 137, 158, 162, 172 and 182.** None of them ever moved more than 0.18% in their favour (I6-001).
6. **Kept, with a sharper split: 143.** The defect is why the trade opened. The loss itself came from a feed gap at exit. While open, the trade was up as much as +1.69%.

Scope: the six frozen IDs are unchanged and none was substituted. Nothing was written to the database, code or config. I did not read the Codex folders, LIVE_TEAM_STATUS.md or LIVE_EVENTS.log. This session has no Write or ExitPlanMode tool, so this output exists only here.

### F-I7-03: The Entry Quality inversion is mostly a volatility mix effect (corrects F-I6-03)
[PROJECT EVIDENCE: I6-002, n = 193; group totals reconcile with I6-003's total of −4.171 USDT, and the 15 s match produced no duplicate rows]

Loss rate by Entry Quality band (columns) within each ATR band (rows):

| ATR band (1m) | 50–54 | 55–59 | 60–69 | 70+ | Whole band: n, loss rate, stop-out rate, net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| below 0.1667% | 43% (n 47) | 33% (n 12) | 43% (n 7) | 33% (n 3) | 69, **41%**, 17%, **−0.12 USDT** |
| 0.1667–0.25% | 33% (n 12) | 67% (n 3) | 42% (n 19) | 63% (n 27) | 61, **51%**, 43%, **+0.08 USDT** |
| 0.25% and above | 50% (n 2) | 67% (n 3) | 69% (n 13) | 67% (n 45) | 63, **67%**, 59%, **−4.13 USDT** |

- **No gradient in the low and high bands.** A gradient remains only in the middle band: 63% loss rate at 70+ against 33–42% in the lower score bands, with small groups.
- **The two scores are entangled.** 77% of the 50–54 scores fall in the low ATR band, and 60% of the 70+ scores fall in the high ATR band. So Entry Quality is acting mostly as a stand-in for volatility.
- **This supports the mechanism I proposed in F-I6-03 (AGENT INFERENCE).** The bands meet at 0.5% stop distance: 3 × 0.1667% and 2 × 0.25% both equal 0.5%.
  - With 1m ATR below 0.25%, the 0.5% stop floor is wider than 2 × ATR. Reward:risk falls below 1 and the −30 penalty applies, so these entries score low but are rarely stopped out.
  - With 1m ATR at 0.25% or more, the stop sits at 2 × ATR. These entries avoid the penalty, score high, and get stopped out 59% of the time.
- **Revised statement:** Entry Quality does not separate good entries from bad ones once volatility is controlled for. The loss driver is entries on high-volatility 1m candles with stops at about 2 × the 1m ATR.
- **Caveat:** I assume `atr_percent` is stored as a percentage (a 0.27% ATR for 143 fits that). I7-002 checks it.

**Self-challenges:**
- **Collinearity.** Stop distance in ATRs largely mirrors the ATR band, because of the stop floor. This is the same effect as F-I7-03, not separate evidence.
- **Scale-ins distort the measure.** `paper_position.stop_loss` is changed only on a progressive add (scale-in). The add raises the stop and averages the entry price (`PaperTradingService.java:263-271`). For those positions, "entry minus stop" no longer measures the original stop distance. I7-003 counts how many positions this affects.
- **"Wider stops would help" is not shown.** A wider stop could just turn stop-outs into larger losses. What matters is whether the price recovered after the stop-out. I7-001 tests this. Until it does, recommendation 4 below stays a hypothesis.
