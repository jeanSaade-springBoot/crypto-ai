# CLAUDE: FTS WINDOW2, Iteration 5 (entry quality, self-review only)

## Summary

The entry-quality review found a structural problem shared by five of the six frozen trades.

- **What the problem is.** On low-volatility 1m candles, the stop is pinned to a fixed 0.5% below the entry price. The profit target is only 3 × ATR (Average True Range, a measure of typical candle size) above it.
- **What it does.** The target ends up closer than the stop: the target is 0.16–0.37% away and the stop 0.5%. Reward:risk is 0.33–0.74.
- **Why nothing caught it.** The Entry Quality score (0–100) takes 30 points off for this, which on its own still leaves a "GOOD" 70. So it can only shrink the position, never block it.
- **Fees.** Paper trading charges no fee. At Binance's standard spot fee (VIP 0, about 0.1% per side, unverified for this account), trade 158's whole target would be eaten by fees.

Trade 143 remains the only confirmed entry defect (F-I4-01). This iteration adds that its ATR entry plan was also out of date. Trade 172 exposes a separate gap: for SHIB, the 1m ATR is smaller than one price tick.

## FINDINGS

### F-I5-01: The stop/target geometry flips reward:risk below 1 on low-volatility 1m candles

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: `AtrRiskService.java:34-45`, `application.yml:137-144`, stored values from I2-007]

**How the code sets the stop and target:**
- Stop distance = max(2 × ATR, 0.5% of price), capped at 8%.
- Target distance = 3 × ATR.
- So whenever ATR is below 0.5% / 3 = **0.1667% of price**, reward:risk = 3 × ATR% / 0.5 and is always below 1.

**The stored values match the defaults exactly.** Each stop is precisely signal price × 0.995 and each target is precisely price + 3 × ATR. That is strong evidence Production ran these defaults. The server's environment overrides are not verified.

| ID | ATR % of price | Target distance | Stop distance | Reward:risk | Win rate needed to break even (no fees) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 137 | 0.107% | 0.322% | 0.500% (floor) | 0.64 | 60.8% |
| 143 | 0.270% | 0.811% | 0.541% (2 × ATR) | 1.50 (1.39 at the 4.033 fill) | — |
| 158 | 0.055% | **0.164%** | 0.500% (floor) | **0.33** | 75.3% |
| 162 | 0.073% | 0.219% | 0.500% (floor) | 0.44 | 69.5% |
| 172 | 0.124% | 0.372% | 0.500% (floor) | 0.74 | 57.3% |
| 182 | 0.080% | 0.241% | 0.500% (floor) | 0.48 | 67.5% |

**The labels and entry plans are consistent with the code.**
- All six signals are labelled LOW volatility, meaning ATR below 0.5% of price (`:139`). That is the normal state for 1m candles, so the label tells you nothing.
- Each trade's ATR entry type matches the strategy thresholds in `AtrRiskService.java:143-174`:
  - 143: BREAKOUT, candle range 4.35 ATR → WAIT_FOR_RETRACEMENT.
  - 162: BREAKOUT, 2.82 ATR → REDUCED_POSITION.
  - 182: trend-following, 2.15 ATR → REDUCED_POSITION.
  - 137, 158 and 172 → STANDARD_ENTRY.

**Caveat (AGENT INFERENCE, carried from F-I3-07):** exits are mostly driven by signals and profit locks, not by the stop or target. So these reward:risk figures describe the plan, not what actually happened. With zero paper fees, trades with reward:risk below 1 did **not** lose more often (F-I3-07). This is a confirmed design weakness, not a demonstrated cause of these losses.

### F-I5-02: Entry Quality cannot block a structurally inverted reward:risk; it only sizes it down

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: `ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:976-1022`, `:1171-1183`, `:229`]

**How the score is built:**
- It starts at 100.
- Reward:risk below 1 costs 30 points (`:991`).
- The block threshold (the "chase cutoff") is 50 (`:229`).
- So an entry with reward:risk 0.33 and no other penalty scores **70 = GOOD_ENTRY**.

**What the guard does with the score:**
- It blocks only below 50.
- Otherwise it caps the size at 50%, 40% or 25%, depending on the score (`:1183`).
- The only exceptions are the BALANCED_EARLY late-entry block (`:672`) and the FIX-119 deferral.

**Where the frozen trades scored** (from the stored explanations, I2-008 and I4-002):

| ID | Entry Quality | Label |
|---|---|---|
| 137 | 58 | |
| 162 | **50** | LATE_ENTRY |
| 172 | 60 | ACCEPTABLE |
| 182 | **50** | |
| 143, 158 | Not visible | The explanation is truncated at 400 characters; DB_REQUEST I5-002 fetches it |

**What can and cannot be derived from the formula** (AGENT INFERENCE, arithmetic on the code's penalty values; the inputs need I5-003):
- **137 (58):** the only combination that gives 58 is 100 − 30 (reward:risk) − 12 (recent-rejection penalty). So the price was within 0.5% of a recent high that buyers had already failed to hold at least twice.
- **172 (60):** 100 − 30 − 10. The 10 is most likely the ATR-extension penalty: two price ticks above the 30-minute low equals 2.94 ATR (see F-I5-05).
- **162 (50) and 182 (50):** both land exactly on the cutoff. Two decompositions are possible:
  - A raw score below 50 that was lifted to 50 by the floor on the rejection penalty (`:1000-1002`). This is exactly the case the FIX-119 code comment describes (`:1234-1236`).
  - Or an ATR-extension penalty of 20.
- **162 does not reconcile.** If the opportunity's best price (0.08675) was used as the reference, the ATR extension is 4.7 ATR, which gives 45, not 50. Either the best price changed after entry, or another input differs. This is a stated uncertainty; I5-003 settles it.

**Conclusion:** four of the six entries sat 0–10 points above the block threshold. The model's single largest penalty, reward:risk, is mechanically present on most 1m low-volatility entries and is never decisive.

### F-I5-04: 143 was a poor entry on what was known at the time; its ATR plan was also out of date

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: I3-001, I2-007, I2-008, `ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:628-640`, `:1472-1514`, `:857-858`]

This strengthens F-I4-01.

**Where the price was:**
- The 1m signal 159408's own plan was WAIT_FOR_RETRACEMENT at 0%. The candle range was 4.35 ATR, inside the BREAKOUT band from 4.25 to 5.25.
- Between 22:27 and 22:34 UTC (01:27–01:34 KSA, 2026-10-12), EGLD 1m prices were 3.972–3.977 (the anchors of the cancelled opportunities 36400–36473).
- The entry at 4.033 was therefore about **+1.5% above the 30-minute low**, at the top of an expansion candle.

**The route trusted an old 5m plan:**
- `SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR` took its "immediate entry at 100%" permission from 5m signal 159285.
- That plan had been computed at a price of **3.986**, 1.2% below the fill.
- The current 5m, 159407, said PULLBACK_ENTRY with immediate entry **not** allowed.
- So both the 5m direction (BUY instead of WATCH) and the 5m risk plan were taken from the superseded candle.

**Two different ATR bases are used for the same entry (new design gap):**
- At decision time, the route scores Entry Quality with the **5m** ATR (`:634`).
- At execution, `revalidateAtExecutionPrice` re-scores it with the **1m** ATR (`:858`).
- For 143, the decision-time cap took the allocation from 30% to 25%. That implies an Entry Quality below 70, even with the more lenient 5m ATR.

**Classification:** confirmed entry defect (F-I4-01), with poor entry quality on information available at the time.

**Outcome, assessed separately:**
- The price peaked at 4.101 (+1.69%).
- The position then exited at 4.027 through the 8-second EGLD price gap (F-I3-02).

### F-I5-05: For SHIB, the 1m ATR is smaller than one price tick, so the ATR-based plan is distorted (172)

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: I2-007, I2-001 / AGENT INFERENCE]

- SHIB prices are stored in steps of 0.00000001 (0.00000548, 0.00000549). One tick is 0.18% of price.
- The ATR was 0.000000006806, which is **0.68 of a tick**.
- In tick terms:
  - The target is 2.04 ticks above entry.
  - The stop is 2.75 ticks below.
  - A one-tick move counts as 1.47 ATR, so a two-tick move already triggers the 2.5-ATR extension penalty.
- 172 entered at 0.00000549 and exited at 0.00000548: a loss of **exactly one tick** (−0.18%).
- **Classification:** a design gap where the ATR plan does not account for tick size. It is not a logic defect. PEPE is likely affected the same way; the sizing in I5-005 will show it.

## Remediation direction (recommendation only, not authorised)

1. Make Entry Quality, or a separate pre-entry gate, account for **target distance after the expected round-trip fee and tick size**. Reject or defer when the net target is at or below 0, or below N ticks.
2. Revisit the stop-floor/target asymmetry. For example, scale the target with the floored stop, or require reward:risk ≥ 1 after the floor for exploratory routes such as FIX-11G and BALANCED_EARLY. This is a trading-rule change and needs a specification and approval.
3. Use **one ATR basis** for the decision-time and execution-time Entry Quality on the setup ATR route.
4. Record `original_decision` in `buy_htf_assessment_audit`. The schema change needs the governed migration path.
5. Unchanged from iteration 4: select the 5m by candle identity and re-check it before execution (F-I4-01).

**Regression checks:**
- Unit tests for the geometry: ATR at 0.05%, 0.1667% and 0.25% of price, checking reward:risk and the gate outcome.
- A SHIB-like tick fixture where ATR is less than one tick.
- 143 should become non-executable when the same-boundary 5m is WATCH or PULLBACK_ENTRY.
- Run Production-parity Replay over the same windows.
