# CLAUDE: FTS WINDOW2, Iteration 6 (root cause and research, self-review only)

## Summary

One of the six frozen trades has a code defect behind it; the other five lost because of weak entry design.

- **Trade 143: confirmed logic defect.** Two analyses race at each 5-minute boundary: the 1m (one-minute candle) analysis and the 5m one. The entry was approved on a 5m BUY from the previous 5-minute candle. A newer 5m WATCH already existed 3.2 s before the fill. Without that, 143 would not have opened.
- **The other five (137, 158, 162, 172, 182): valid losing trades with weak entries.** Each bought into short-term noise, either at the top of a narrow 30-minute range or on a bounce inside a falling run. Each then closed on the next bearish signal. All five losses were between −0.13% and −0.34%, which is ordinary 1m noise.
- **Ruled out:**
  - Stale data: entries were made 3 s after the signal.
  - Execution delay: the fill was within one tick of the decision price on all six.
  - Stop-loss behaviour: none of the six hit its stop.
  - Fees: paper fees are 0.

There is also a new population-level finding. Across all completed paper trades, a higher Entry Quality score went with a higher loss rate, not a lower one. That fits the I5-005 per-symbol view: most losses come from stop-outs on more volatile symbols, and the study's selection rule (status `CLOSED` only, no `STOPPED`) can never pick those trades.

### F-I6-02: Five of the six bought short-term noise and exited on the next bearish signal

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: I5-003, I1-003. AGENT INFERENCE on the mechanism]

The table places each entry inside the 30-minute range of 1m closing prices before it. These are `latest_price` close values, not candle highs and lows.

| ID | 30-min close range | Entry position in range | Loss in 1m ATR multiples | Signals in the 30 min before entry |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 172 | 0.00000547–0.00000549 | **100%** (top) | 1.5 | Mix of SELL and STRONG_SELL, then BUY |
| 162 | 0.08672–0.08705 | **100%** (new window high) | 4.7 | SELL streak at 03:36–03:40 UTC, then WATCH |
| 143 | 3.972–4.032 | **Above the range** | 0.55 | STRONG_SELL 22:26–22:34 UTC, then BUY, then a breakout |
| 158 | 1.4084–1.4115 | 77% | 2.3 | NEUTRAL/WATCH, with 3 BUYs |
| 182 | 0.1875–0.1895, falling | 45%, +0.48% off the low | 2.6 | **16 consecutive STRONG_SELLs** from 09:39 to 09:54 UTC |
| 137 | 3.992–4.009 | 41% | 2.8 | **10 STRONG_SELLs** from 20:42 to 20:53 UTC |

Three things the six entries share:
- **Each loss was small next to the entry's own noise.** Losses were 0.13–0.34%, which is 0.55–4.7 × the 1m ATR.
- **Half the entries were made while the current 1m signal was WATCH, not BUY:** 143, 158 and 162. The setup, wakeup and accumulated-evidence routes let execution go ahead without a current 1m BUY.
- **The 1m decision flips within one or two minutes.**
  - 137: STRONG_SELL at 20:53, WATCH at 20:54, STRONG_BUY at 20:55 UTC.
  - 143: STRONG_SELL at 22:34, BUY at 22:35 UTC.
  - 182: STRONG_SELL at 09:54, NEUTRAL at 09:55, BUY at 09:56 UTC.
  - So a 1m "STRONG" label does not mean the move will persist (AGENT INFERENCE).

Execution delay and stale data are ruled out (PROJECT EVIDENCE):
- The decision price and the fill price matched to within one tick: 143 decided at 4.032 and filled at 4.033; 158 decided at 1.4109 and filled at 1.4108; the other four matched exactly.
- Every fill came about 3 s after the 1m signal.

### F-I6-03: Population data — higher Entry Quality went with *more* losses

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: I5-004, n = 193 completed positions]

| Entry Quality band | n | Loss rate | Stop-out rate | Average PnL (USDT) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50–54 | 61 | 41% | 23% | +0.011 |
| 55–59 | 18 | 44% | 28% | −0.029 |
| 60–69 | 39 | 51% | 41% | −0.020 |
| 70+ | 75 | **64%** | **53%** | −0.048 |

The loss *rate* rises with the score, so this is not just bigger positions losing more USDT. Higher scores do get larger allowed sizes (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:1183`), but the rate comparison is independent of size.

A likely mechanism (AGENT INFERENCE, consistent with I5-005; I6-002 and I6-003 test it):
- The model's largest penalty is −30 points for reward:risk below 1 (`:991`).
- That penalty hits low-volatility entries, where the 0.5% stop floor puts the stop well beyond 2 × ATR. Those entries are rarely stopped out.
- More volatile entries escape the penalty and score 70+. Their stop sits at exactly 2 × the 1m ATR, close enough for normal noise to hit it.
- I5-005 matches this pattern:
  - The symbols where most entries had reward:risk below 1 are near breakeven (XLM, XRP, DOGE, SOL, ONG, ETH, SHIB).
  - The worst symbols are the more volatile PEPE (−3.01 USDT, 13 of 26 stopped), EDU (−1.21, 17 of 29 stopped) and ENA (−1.16, 13 of 22 stopped). PEPE had no entries with reward:risk below 1.

Caveats:
- `wallet_managed_position` is matched to `paper_position` by symbol and opening time within 15 s. A scale-in could produce a wrong match.
- The PnL figures are in USDT, not percent. I6-002 recomputes them in percent.

### F-I6-04: Corrections to iteration 5 after I5-001 and I5-002

[PROJECT EVIDENCE: I5-001, I5-002, I5-006, `PaperTradingService.java:349-384`]

**Where the stored score comes from.** The stored `entry_quality_score` is recomputed at the fill price, on the 1m signal (`PaperTradingService.java:377-378`). It is not the decision-time score.

**Execution-time scores:**

| ID | Score |
|---|---|
| 137 | 58 |
| 143 | 70 |
| 158 | 50 |
| 162 | 50 |
| 172 | 60 |
| 182 | 50 |

**Correction to F-I5-04 (trade 143):**
- At decision time, opportunity 36524 scored **60**, using the 5m ATR. The earlier, cancelled opportunity 36481 scored 50.
- At execution, the score was 70, using the 1m ATR.
- The code comment says the 5m ATR is used "so a tiny 1m ATR cannot manufacture" a chase verdict. Here the reverse happened. The 1m ATR at 22:40 UTC (0.010899) was **larger** than the ATR of the old 5m signal 159285 (0.009073).
- So the reason the 5m ATR is preferred did not hold in this case. The point that two different ATR bases are used still stands.

**Three of the six execution-time scores were exactly 50, the block threshold (158, 162 and 182).**
- One way to get exactly 50 is the clamp at `:1000-1002`. When the recent-rejection penalty is applied to a score that was already 50 or more, the code lifts the result back to 50.
- Whether that clamp is what happened here is AGENT INFERENCE. The score's components are not stored in the database (U-I6-2).

**Allocation:**
- The stored allocations of 15% for 162 and 182 come from `atrPercent × executionPercent` (`PaperTradingService.java:359-360`): a REDUCED_POSITION ATR plan at 60% times the 25% Entry Quality cap.
- This is consistent with the code and is not a defect.

### F-I6-05: Research context

[EXTERNAL RESEARCH, weighted cautiously]

- **Short-term reversals.** One-minute Bitcoin data shows systematic intraday reversals, larger after bigger moves ([Ledger: On the Intraday Behavior of Bitcoin](https://ledger.pitt.edu/ojs/ledger/article/view/213)).
- **Bid-ask bounce.** Minute-level sampling picks up bid-ask bounce, which creates autocorrelation between consecutive prices ([arXiv 2004.00550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.00550)).
- **Asymmetry.** Reversion is stronger after negative returns ([Corbet & Katsiampa, SHU](https://shura.shu.ac.uk/23470/)).
- **Possible artefact.** A recent cross-market preprint reports that apparent short-horizon crypto predictability largely disappears once a one-bar gap is enforced between inputs and outcome ([arXiv 2608.21888](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.21888)). Its date and peer-review status are unverified.
- **Fees.** Binance's standard spot fee (VIP 0) is about 0.10% maker and 0.10% taker, with 25% off when paid in BNB. These figures come from third-party sources ([CryptoPotato](https://cryptopotato.com/binance-fees/), [Traders Union](https://tradersunion.com/brokers/crypto/view/binance/fees/), [Bitget Academy](https://www.bitget.cloud/academy/binance-fees-compare-1)). The tier for this account is still unverified (U-I2-7).

What this means for the study (AGENT INFERENCE):
- Buying the top of a micro-range (162, 172), or just after a 1m expansion (143), runs into exactly the reversal behaviour these studies describe.
- Bounce entries (137, 182) are the opposite case: they bet on reversion after a decline. Both exits came as the 5m decline continued.
- At a 0.2% round-trip fee, the six losses would roughly double. Population wins below 0.2% would become losses (sized by I6-005).

## Root-cause verdict (draft, to be frozen in iterations 7–8)

1. **Confirmed defect (143):** at the boundary, the 5m approval is chosen by analysis completion time, not by candle identity, and it is never re-read before execution.
2. **Five valid losing trades.** The common cause is entry-timing weakness: the system admits entries with no edge against short-term noise. Four design conditions contribute:
   - Execution is allowed while the current 1m signal is not a BUY.
   - The higher-timeframe approvals are permissive: a masked 5m, a 5m with `final_entry_allowed=0`, a single-BUY exemption, and a 1h trend score one point above its threshold.
   - Entry Quality cannot block, and it sat exactly at the threshold three times.
   - The stop and target geometry, and the fee blindness, are design weaknesses but are not proven causes of these losses.
3. **Study-level bias:** the `CLOSED`-only selection leaves out stop-outs, where the population's losses concentrate. The data so far points to tight 2 × ATR stops on more volatile symbols as the main population loss mechanism, which no frozen trade represents.

## Remediation direction (recommendation only, not authorised)

1. **Trade 143:** select the 5m approval by candle identity (the FIX-141 pattern) and re-check it before execution. Unchanged from iteration 4.
2. **Require a non-bearish, current 1m BUY at execution** on the setup, wakeup and accumulated-evidence routes, or justify an exemption explicitly in a specification.
3. **Rework Entry Quality** so the reward:risk penalty does not reward the volatility profile that gets stopped out. Add a range-location or recent-reversal term. Recalibrate after I6-002 and I6-003.
4. **Base the stop distance on noise** for volatile symbols (for example, a stop measured on the 5m ATR, or a multiplier floor), and validate it in Replay. This is a trading-rule change and needs the governed specification process.
5. **Widen future study selection** to `status IN ('CLOSED','STOPPED')`.

Regression checks:
- Same-boundary 1m-before-5m and 5m-before-1m cases on every route.
- Entry Quality calibration against a held-out set of positions.
- Production-parity Replay over EGLD 2026-10-11 22:30–22:45 UTC and XRP 2026-10-12 02:30–03:31 UTC.
