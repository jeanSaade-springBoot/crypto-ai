## 1. Root causes

### RC-1 (78, 91, 96, 104): the same hourly SELL is soft at entry and forces the exit
*CONFIRMED in code; DB I3-012, I4-008, I5-002.*

- **At entry:** `HigherTimeframeBuyAssessmentService.java:33-34,47` turns any quality-valid 1h SELL into NEUTRAL and caps the entry at 25%.
- **At exit:** `PositionExitPolicy.java:25-32` exits on "1h bearish and 5m not supportive". It uses the newest 1h signal with no check on its age or on when the position opened (`LivePositionProtectionService.java:95-97`). The Javadoc (`:14`) promises a "fresh" 1h SELL.
- **Why it loses by construction:** entry needs the first 5m flip to BUY, so the first 5m bar that isn't BUY or WATCH closes the trade. No new 1h signal arrived during any of these four holds.
- **Business effect:** 4 of 5 SELL-context entries since V107 lost, net −0.1635 USDT.

### RC-2 (82): weak entry geometry plus the restart defect
*CONFIRMED in code and DB.*

- **Weak entry:** a 0.5% stop floor with a 3-ATR target produced a reward/risk of 0.45 (F-24). The −12 penalty clamp held the entry-quality score at exactly 50 when it would otherwise have blocked the entry (F-23).
- **Restart defect:** the startup candle import closed open candles without analysing them (F-21), so the hour-boundary candle was never analysed and the entry ran on a 63-minute-old 1h signal. Whether that changed the decision is **not proven**.

### RC-3 (101): exit policy, not the entry
- The trade reached its target. After the FIX-118 rebase, it exited on the hard floor (F-13).

### Contributing factors in all six trades (policy weaknesses, not defects)
- No minimum reward/risk on the direct, accumulated-evidence and wake-up routes (F-24).
- One tick of fill drift is accepted without re-anchoring the stop and target (F-25).
- No fee or tick-size awareness (F-26).
- A WATCH signal exactly at the support threshold of 60 carried the order in 78 and 91 (F-28).

## 2. New findings

### F-30: the restart outage left a breached stop unacted on
*PROJECT EVIDENCE: I4-005, I5-005.*

- **The breach:** UNI position 80 had a stop of 7.628665. Price traded at 7.624 in the 18:00 KSA (15:00 UTC) minute while no price events were arriving (outage 17:59:06–18:02:21 KSA).
- **The late exit:** the stop actually executed at 18:15:47 KSA (15:15:47 UTC), at 7.627.
- **Financial effect:** close to nothing this time, because the exit price was about the same.
- **Why it matters anyway:** the outage-window candles were later filled in as BACKFILL rows. That repairs the candle history but not the protection, because nothing replays protection over backfilled lows. This is a real occurrence of the `AGENTS.md` release gate on protecting open positions during application replacement.
- **AERO position 81 is inconclusive.** Its stored stop of 0.908416 had already been crossed by a *live* candle at 17:58 KSA (low 0.9080), yet the stop never fired. Two possible reasons:
  - the active stop in `wallet_managed_position.stop_loss_usdt`, which drives protection (`LivePositionProtectionService.java:174`), differed from the stored paper stop; or
  - protection only evaluates the ~2-second kline price updates, so short wicks between updates are never seen.
- I6-001 and I6-002 test which one it was. If it is the second, **every stop is exposed to unseen wicks**. That is an outcome-fidelity issue for paper trading and a parity issue for Replay, which works from candle closes.

### F-31: in three of the four RC-1 trades, the hourly SELL meant "near the top of the range"
*PROJECT EVIDENCE: I5-002; the interpretation is AGENT INFERENCE.*

- The 1h signals for 78, 91 and 96 came from RANGE_MEAN_REVERSION in a RANGE regime. A SELL from that strategy means price is near the upper band.
- The system entered a 1m TREND_FOLLOWING BUY into that range top, on a 5m flip with confidence 60–68.
- So the soft-conflict cap treats a structural "you are at resistance" signal as neutral. The entry-quality score has no hourly-range term, which matches the F-29 observation for 104.

### F-32: the entry-quality score does not track outcomes
*PROJECT EVIDENCE: I5-003. This is outcome-based prevalence and is not used to judge any entry.*

| Band | n | Losses | Net PnL (USDT) |
|---|---|---|---|
| EQ 85+ | 7 | 3 | **+1.24** |
| EQ 70–84 | 16 | **14** | **−2.17** |
| EQ 55–69 | 21 | 10 | −0.53 |
| EQ < 55 | 28 | 12 | −0.10 |
| RR ≥ 1.25 | 24 | 18 (75%) | −0.94 |
| RR < 1 | 33 | 15 (45%) | −0.25 |

- **This partly revises F-24:** a reward/risk floor alone would not have cut losses on this sample. Trades with a high signal reward/risk lost more often, because their targets are farther away and these exits cut them early.
- **Effect on the root cause:** the losses come mainly from the **exit policy (RC-1) and calibration**, not from reward/risk alone.
- **Caveats:**
  - one day of data (n = 72);
  - the bands use the *signal* reward/risk, not the reward/risk at the fill;
  - the join to `wallet_managed_position` is assumed to be one-to-one.

## 3. External research

- **Spring startup order.** Spring Boot calls `ApplicationRunner`s in order and then publishes `ApplicationReadyEvent`. This supports F-21's premise that the WebSocket starts only after the startup import has finished. Sources: [reflectoring.io](https://reflectoring.io/spring-boot-execute-on-startup/), [cnblogs (Spring source walk-through)](https://www.cnblogs.com/FraserYu/p/12117888.html), [codemia](https://codemia.io/knowledge-hub/path/running_code_after_spring_boot_starts). One source calls it "asynchronous"; that claim looks wrong ([mastertheboss](https://mastertheboss.com/spring-boot/getting-started-with-spring-boot/spring-boot-quickstarts/how-to-execute-a-java-class-at-spring-boot-startup/)).
- **Binance fees.** Most sources agree on 0.10% spot taker for regular users, or 0.075% with the 25% BNB discount. I didn't reach the official page, so this is **still unverified**, and F-26 keeps its 0.075–0.10% sensitivity range. Sources: [hijri-calendars](https://hijri-calendars.com/blog/binance-fees-explained-2026?lang=en), [intercom](https://intercom.help/cryptoreferralcodes/en/articles/15054844).
- **Multi-timeframe practice.** Practitioner sources describe the higher timeframe as a gate on entries and recommend using the last *completed* higher-timeframe value. I found no peer-reviewed evidence either way. Supporting the RC-1 diagnosis is AGENT INFERENCE: an hourly signal that is overridden at entry but authoritative at exit contradicts that common framing. Sources: [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/higher-timeframe-trend-filter/), [QuantifiedStrategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/multi-timeframe-analysis-and-strategy).
- **Prior external work, still valid:** Binance kline streams push updates about every 2 s ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/binance-spot-api-docs/web-socket-streams)). This is the basis for the wick hypothesis in F-30.
