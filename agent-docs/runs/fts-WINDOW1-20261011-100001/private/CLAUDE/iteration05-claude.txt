# Claude, failed-trade study WINDOW1, iteration 5: entry quality

All six entries followed the rules, and every input was fresh. Judged only on what the system knew at the moment of entry, four of the six were weak trades:

- **Trade 82** had a reward/risk of 0.45. It was admitted at exactly the minimum entry-quality score of 50, because a rule clamps the score there.
- **Trades 91 and 78** were entered on 1m WATCH signals sitting exactly at the support threshold (score 60), with low volume. Their reward/risk at the fill price was below 1.
- **Trade 104** bought into an over-extended volume spike while the hourly signal was SELL.

The other two entries were sound:

- **Trade 96** had a decent signal, but its stop and target were only 2–3 price ticks away.
- **Trade 101** was a good entry. It reached its target and was lost by the exit policy, not the entry.

## 2. FINDINGS

### F-23: a clamp kept trade 82 alive at the entry-quality cutoff
*CONFIRMED in code; that the clamp was binding is AGENT INFERENCE from arithmetic.*

- **The rule:** `ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:995-1003` applies the recent-rejection penalty (−12, `:236`) but never lets it push the score below `CHASE_ENTRY_CUTOFF=50` (`:229`) when the score was at or above 50 before the penalty. Any score below 50 is blocked as a chase entry (`:1173-1180`).
- **What 82's evidence says:** opportunity 24429 records "Entry Quality 50/100 (LATE_ENTRY); recent rejection zone near 0.198600" (I2-006).
- **Why the clamp must have fired:** a natural result of exactly 50 needs a pre-penalty score of 62. With the penalty set used here (all penalties are multiples of 5, plus 22 and 12), 62 can't be produced. So the pre-penalty score was between 50 and 61, and the clamp lifted the result to 50. Without the clamp, 82 would have been blocked as a chase entry.
- **What the system knew at entry:**
  - reward/risk 0.453, which alone costs −30 (`:991`);
  - entry at 0.1984, just under a twice-rejected high of 0.1986, with the target (0.19885) above that high;
  - an 8-minute-old 5m and a 63-minute-old 1h, because the hour-boundary candle was silently closed by the startup import (F-21).
- **Classification:** followed the FIX-091 Fix-7 design ("penalty only, never a hard veto"). It is a **policy weakness:** the clamp neutralises the one quality signal that would have stopped the weakest entry in the set.

### F-24: the 0.5% stop floor with a 3-ATR target pushes reward/risk below 1, and nothing enforces a minimum on these routes
*CONFIRMED in code and DB.*

- **How the geometry works:**
  - stop = max(2·ATR, 0.5% of price) (`AtrRiskService.java:34-45`, `application.yml:143`);
  - target = 3·ATR;
  - so whenever ATR% < 0.25%, reward/risk = 3·ATR% / 0.5. It drops below 1 when ATR% < 0.167%.
- **It matches the data exactly** (I1-002): 82: 3×0.0755/0.5 = 0.453; 91: 3×0.0910/0.5 = 0.546; 104: 3×0.1818/0.5 = 1.09; 96: 3×0.2189/0.5 = 1.31. Four of six frozen trades had a floored stop, and every one was classed "Volatility LOW".
- **Where reward/risk actually gates entry:**
  - In the entry-quality score it is only a penalty: −30 below 1, −15 below 1.25 (`:991-992`).
  - A hard 1:1 minimum exists only on the deferred-continuation route (`:1756-1764`).
  - The direct, accumulated-evidence and wake-up routes reach `applyInitialEntryQualityGuard`, which blocks only scores below 50.
  - Other reward/risk thresholds exist at `:1055/:1098/:1128/:1539/:1631` for other routes. I didn't verify whether they apply to these entries.
- **Correction to my F-11:** reward/risk is not "display only". It is a soft score input, but not a gate on these paths.
- **Classification:** policy weakness or design gap, not an implementation defect.

### F-25: price drift at execution lowers reward/risk without re-anchoring the stop and target
*CONFIRMED in code; PROJECT EVIDENCE for trade 78.*

- **The rule:** `revalidateAtExecutionPrice` (`:1150-1168`) rejects a fill only when price is at or above the target, at or below the stop, or entry quality falls below 50. The stop and target stay anchored to the signal.
- **What happened in 78:** wallet fill 195 executed at 4.06e-6 against a decision price of 4.05e-6, a 1-tick (0.247%) drift. That cut reward/risk from 1.50 to 0.76, and the stored entry quality fell to 60. The entry still passed.
- **Classification:** followed the rules. The guard lets one tick of drift halve reward/risk on coarse-tick coins.

### F-26: neither the entry-quality score nor reward/risk accounts for fees or tick size
*PROJECT EVIDENCE for the code and DB; EXTERNAL RESEARCH for the fee rate.*

- **No fees:** fees are recorded as 0 (F-12). Even so, at roughly 0.1% per side, trades 82 and 91 needed about 96% and 91% win rates to break even. Their targets (0.227% and 0.273%) barely cover a 0.2% round trip.
- **No tick size:** no tick-size handling exists (F-10). Trade 96 scored entry quality **100** with its stop 2.0 ticks and its target 2.7 ticks away. Trade 91 scored 70 with a target 1.5 ticks away.
- **Fee rate is unverified:** older sources give Binance's regular-user spot fee as 0.10% maker / 0.10% taker, or 0.075% when paid in BNB. A newer report mentions zero maker fees and reduced taker fees. Market fills are taker fills either way. The exact current rate is **unverified**, so I treat 0.075–0.10% per side as a sensitivity range.

### F-27: the soft-conflict entries were counter-trend first-bounce trades labelled as trend-following
*PROJECT EVIDENCE for the facts; AGENT INFERENCE for the interpretation.*

- **Prior bearish streaks:** in 78, 91 and 104, the opportunity history just before entry is a run of `BEARISH_REVERSAL` cancellations confirmed by 5m SELL/STRONG_SELL (I2-006: 6, 10 and 2 cancellations). Entry came on the **first** 5m flip to BUY (wake-up requires the previous 5m to be non-bullish, `:408-412`) while the 1h stayed SELL with confidence 70.
- **Strategy label comes from the 1m:** the 1m strategy was TREND_FOLLOWING in a 1m WEAK_UPTREND regime. In the multi-timeframe step, "direction is mixed; no trend-following adjustment was applied".
- **Wake-up ignored a recent quality warning:** in 91, consolidation flagged `QUALITY_DECAY` at 21:38:02 KSA (audit 1312: average confidence 61, score falling from 83 to 80). The wake-up approved the entry 2 minutes later (audit 1318). The wake-up's eligible statuses are BUILDING, WEAKENING, BLOCKED and CONFIRMED (`:414-418`). It doesn't re-check consolidation quality; whether `QUALITY_DECAY` changes the opportunity's status at all is **unknown**.
- **Interpretation:** this is the same structure as F-20 seen from the entry side. A one-bar 5m flip against a standing 1h SELL is admitted, and the next 5m flip back exits it.

### F-28: a WATCH signal sitting exactly at the support threshold carried the order in 78 and 91
*CONFIRMED in code and DB.*

- **The rule:** `isSupportiveCurrentSignal` accepts a WATCH with score ≥ 60 and confidence ≥ 60 (`:73-74, :2214-2219`). Both 1m signals scored exactly 60.
- **Volume:** both had below-average volume (RVOL 0.40 and 0.56). In 91 the 1m signal's own multi-timeframe read said 5m=NEUTRAL; the 5m BUY arrived 1.4 s later.
- **Classification:** followed the rules, and these were marginal-quality executions.

### F-29: trade 104 chased an over-extended spike
*PROJECT EVIDENCE.*

- **The signal:** a 1m BREAKOUT BUY with RVOL 15.25, RSI 67.95, price 2.91 ATR above SMA20 and funding at the 100th percentile (crowded longs). The 1h was SELL with confidence 70.
- **The routing:** the direct 1m route rejected the entry (audit 1558, 5m=WATCH). 0.9 s later the 5m candle closed BUY and the wake-up approved the **same** 1m signal 133249 (audit 1560). New 5m information legitimately changed the outcome, so this is not an inconsistency.
- **Sizing:** entry quality 65 and the strategy's REDUCED_POSITION (60%) brought the size down to 15%. The protections worked as designed, but nothing in the entry quality considers the hourly direction.

### F-22 / trade 101: the entry was not the cause
*PROJECT EVIDENCE.*

- **At entry:** score 83, RVOL 2.44, reward/risk 1.55, and an ask wall 1.04% above, beyond the target. The 1h "BUY" had confidence 49 and was entry-blocked (F-22, still open).
- **Weaker points:** the opportunity was 7 h old (the age penalty is capped at −15), and the initial entry quality was 50.
- **The add:** the 35% add filled 0.24% lower, at 0.9481, which lowered the average entry.
- **Outcome:** the trade reached its target, then lost through the FIX-118 rebase (F-13). The entry was valid.
