- **[AI, strong]** The app and the database are on one machine with one clock. That clock runs 35–556 ms **behind** Binance, and the gap moves by hundreds of ms within minutes.

### F3-3 [PE]: price-age checks correct for the clock gap; the audit timestamps do not

**Corrected checks:**
- Price acceptance (`MarketPriceEventService.java:39-44`) uses the Binance-corrected clock: future skew ≤2 s, maximum age 15 s.
- Analysis eligibility (`CandleClosedAnalysisWorker.java:60-62, 150, 160`) and the closed-candle detector also use the corrected clock.
- So the ~0.1–0.5 s gap could not wrongly accept or reject prices or analysis. **No trading effect.**

**Uncorrected timestamps:**
- A live signal's `generated_at` is the raw app clock (`AnalysisService.java:150`).
- Database `created_at` defaults are the raw database clock.
- Visible effect: signal 156820 has `generated_at` 20:58:59.999003, essentially the candle's close boundary. Signal 166141 has `generated_at` 03:02:59.987221 (10-12 00:02:59.987), 12 ms *before* the 1m close it appears to analyse (to be confirmed in I3-001).
- Opportunity rows (`execution_opportunity`) also start before the minute boundary: 20:52:59.987, 21:00:59.972 (I2-009).

**[AI]** Any latency worked out from these audit columns, including the worker's own `liveCloseToGenerationMs` log field (`CandleClosedAnalysisWorker.java:216-221`), is understated by up to ~0.56 s. A timestamp that seems to come before the candle closes is clock lag, not look-ahead. **It is a forensic-accuracy gap, not a trading defect.**

### F3-5 [PE]: the exits that waited for a price update took 4.2–43.5 s after the deciding candle closed

| ID | Exit path | Deciding candle close | Wallet SELL | Close → fill | Price at signal → fill |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 137 | waited for update | 20:59:59.999 (17:59:59.999) | 21:00:08.935 | 8.94 s | 3.988 → 3.987 |
| 158 | waited for update | 03:29:59.999 (00:29:59.999) | 03:30:04.217 | 4.22 s | 1.4089 → 1.409 |
| 162 | signal (immediate) | 04:25:59.999 (01:25:59.999) | 04:26:04.134 | 4.14 s | filled at the signal's own price, 0.08675 |
| 172 | waited for update | 07:14:59.999 (04:14:59.999) | 07:15:43.492 | **43.49 s** | 5.48e-6, unchanged |

- The delay cost nothing in these six: about −0.001 for 137 and 0 for the rest.
- **[AI]** Two exit styles exist. Signal exits (162) fill at once at the signal's price. The others wait for the next Binance price update, so in thinly traded symbols the delay has no upper bound. That is a design property, to be confirmed by code in Iteration 4.

- The time from `generated_at` to the row's `created_at` is about 1.3 s for most symbols, but 1.7–2.8 s for ACE (176857: 2.30 s; 176881: 2.80 s; 176792: 2.59 s).
- **[AI]** The extra ACE time likely comes from enrichment work, such as liquidity or derivatives REST calls. That is unproven; I3-001 asks for `liquidity_evaluated_at` and `derivatives_evaluated_at`.
- Exits for 137, 158, 172 and 182 were all decided on 5-minute boundaries.

### F3-7 [PE + AI]: on 5-minute boundaries, the 1m entry decision races the 5m analysis of the same boundary

- **137:** the 5m signal used, 156721, has a lower ID than the 1m signal 156723, so it was inserted first. The entry saw the current 5m signal.
- **143:** 5m WATCH 159407 was inserted 8 ms before 1m 159408 (I1 F2-7). The entry still cited the previous 5m BUY 159285.
- **158:** the wake-up came from 5m BUY 165415, generated 23 ms after the 1m WATCH.
- **172 and 182:** not on a boundary.
- **[AI]** 3 of the 6 entries happened on boundaries where the result depends on which thread commits first. This is the timing form of candidate F-18. Replay must order these signals the way production committed them, or parity is not guaranteed (U2-9).
- I3-001 asks for `confluence_higher_signal_generated_at`, which shows which 5m signal each 1m confluence actually read.

### F3-8 [PE + ER + AI]: price-update gaps come from the Binance feed, not from the app dropping updates (partly resolves U2-7)

**Code:**
- Every 1m kline message becomes a price event. The observed time is Binance's event time `E`, and the event type is set by `canonical="1m"` (`BinanceKlineService.java:94-106`).
- An unchanged price is not filtered out: 143 shows 4.056 at both 22:40:23 and 22:40:25 (I2-004).
- Only messages with the same timestamp are rejected (`MarketPriceEventService.java:66-72`).

**External:** Binance documents a 2000 ms update speed for kline streams other than 1s. I found no source saying updates are skipped when nothing trades.

**Data:**
- EGLD (143) price updates arrive in about 2 s steps, with gaps of 4–14 s (e.g. 22:43:05 → 22:43:19 → 22:43:27 → 22:43:35).
- In the candles, ACE 09:51 and 09:53 and SHIB 06:32 and 06:34 had 0–1 trades.

**[AI]:** the gaps are the exchange's own update rate plus quiet markets. Whether Binance skips updates when nothing changes is still open; I3-007 tests this.

### F3-9 [PE]: at each candle close, the single WebSocket thread queues price updates by about 40–100 ms

- All ingestion runs on `WebSocketClient-SecureIO-1` (log), with candle, price and protection transactions done inline (`BinanceKlineService.java:101-111`).
- For 143, the recorded latency (`created_at − observed_at`) is about 0–10 ms mid-minute. At candle closes it was 85 ms (22:41:00), 48 ms (22:42:00) and 104 ms (22:43:00).
- **[AI]** This is head-of-line delay while about 21 symbols × 3 intervals close at once. It is immaterial at this size and not a defect.
