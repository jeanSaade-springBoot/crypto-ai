**Code path:**
- `ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:1485` looks up the 5m with `latestAtOrBefore(current, …)`.
- That lookup is bounded by `generatedAt <= 1m.generatedAt` (`:2455-2461`, `:2608`), so #159407 was excluded even though it had been saved 175 ms before the BUY.

- The counterfactual (no entry at all) is AGENT INFERENCE. Another entry route might still have qualified.

**Context:** wallet position 171 had sold at 3.996 on a single-tick dip 22 s earlier (I2-008, I1-008). Trade 143 then re-entered 0.93% higher, and price fell to 4.022–4.026 within 6–8 s of entry.

### I3-F03 — Production and Replay pick a different 5m signal at 5-minute boundaries (PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE)

**Production:**
- `CandleAnalysisDispatcher` uses one independent lane per symbol and interval (`CandleAnalysisDispatcher.java:34-35,102-110`). There is no ordering between the 1m and 5m lanes.
- Each signal's `generated_at` is the wall-clock time at analysis start, and the signal is saved about 1.3 s later (`AnalysisService.java:147-153`).
- Two lookups therefore see different 5m signals:
  - Confluence, during analysis, sees only 5m signals that are already **saved** (`MultiTimeframeConfluenceService.java:105-112`). Trade 137 shows this: 5m #156721 started 17 ms *before* the 1m but was saved later (20:55:02.887), so confluence still used the older #156594 (I2-001/002).
  - Execution sees only 5m signals with `generated_at <= 1m.generated_at`.

**Replay:**
- Signals are processed in close-time order with interval order 1h → 5m → 1m (`RegressionTestWorker.java:557-560,787-793`).
- All signals are timed at candle close (`:584`), so `<=` always includes the 5m that closed at the same moment.

**How often (DBQ-CLAUDE-I2-006):**
- 86 executed BUYs since 10-01 sat on a 5m boundary.
- About 40 of the roughly 45 not triggered by a fresh 5m (the wake-up route) had the 5m of the just-closed candle generated after the 1m. Those entries used the *previous* 5m signal.
- Within that group, three SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR entries had a *new* 5m of WATCH: #119 ENA, #255 ONG and #358 (trade 143). This route requires a bullish 5m, so Replay would most likely not have entered on it (AGENT INFERENCE).
- Some gaps are only 1–2 ms (#255, #118). The ordering is effectively a coin toss.

**Replay can reproduce:**
- 143's *exit*, since it is tick-driven.
- 137, 158, 162, 172 and 182, since their 5m inputs either matched or did not change the decision.

**Replay most likely cannot reproduce:**
- 143's *entry*. Replay sees the current 5m, the opposite of Production's choice.

This breaks the documented "Replay = Production" rule (for example `TradeExecutionValidationService.java:209-211`).

### I3-F04 — Trade 143's exit came from a real one-second market sweep (EXTERNAL RESEARCH + PROJECT EVIDENCE)

**Binance trades** for UTC 19:43:25–19:43:35 (KSA 22:43:25–35):
- 2 trades at 4.100 at :26.
- **No trades from :27 to :33.**
- 86 trades at :34, from 4.097 down to 4.025.
- 34 trades at :35, between 4.024 and 4.040.

**Our ticks:**
- 4.100 at 22:43:27.038, then 4.027 at 22:43:35.021 (I1-008).
- Each tick was saved 2–35 ms after its observation time.
- Other symbols kept ticking during the gap: EDU at :30, SHIB at :33 (I2-005; event IDs 1789382 → 1789409, with 26 inserts for other symbols in between).
- EGLD's earlier quiet periods behave the same way (14 s at 22:43:05–19; 20 s at 20:54:39–59 with an unchanged price).

**Conclusion:**
- The 8-second tick gap is genuine silence on the exchange.
- My working inference is that the kline stream pushes about every 2 s only when the candle changes. Binance's documentation states the 2000 ms update speed, but not the change-only behaviour.
- The entire move through the lock (4.0758), the hard floor (4.0350) and entry (4.033) happened within one second.
- With 2-second kline cadence, no protection logic could have exited above entry. The rule did what it was designed to do.

**Answers U2-04:** there was no ingestion pause.

### I3-F05 — Trades 172 and 182 matched the coded 5m rules (PROJECT EVIDENCE)

**How the check works:**
- `validateBuy` → `balancedBuyWithNeutralFiveException` → `balancedBuy(five, one)` (`TradeExecutionValidationService.java:101-107,218-221,300-317`).
- It receives **only the decision enums**. Neither the 5m signal's `final_entry_allowed` nor its `original_decision` is consulted.

**Trade 172:**
- 5m #171559 was BUY with `final_entry_allowed=0` (blocked by RANGE_ENTRY_LOCATION).
- `isBullish(5m)` plus a NEUTRAL 1h gives `BALANCED_STRONG` (`:306-308`), which matches opportunity #39062.

**Trade 182:**
- 5m #176655 was NEUTRAL (originally STRONG_SELL, score 25), with a WATCH 1h.
- That gives `BALANCED_NEUTRAL_5M_WATCH_1H` (`:263-283`), which matches opportunity #40290.

**Inconsistency:**
- The exceptional-probe route *does* veto on a bearish `originalDecision` (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:1368-1369`). The direct BUY route does not.
- Replay's authority comparison treats "original bearish, neutralised" as intended (`RegressionTestWorker.java:250-253`).
- I classify this as **design inconsistency / strategy weakness, not a defect**.

**Caveat:** the current tree includes `htfAssessment` in `validateBuy` (`:93-98`). The build that ran on 10-11/12 is not proven (U3-03).
