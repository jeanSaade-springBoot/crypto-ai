## FINDINGS

### F4-1: Trade 143 entered on a 5m BUY that a newer 5m WATCH had replaced 2 ms later [PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE]

**The data** [PROJECT EVIDENCE: I1-004, I2-005]:
- The 1m signal 159408 (candle 22:39) was generated at 22:40:01.634.
- The 5m candle 22:35–22:39:59.999 closed at the same instant. Its signal **159407 = WATCH** was generated at **22:40:01.636**, which is 2 ms later and 1.5 s before the fill at 22:40:03.115.
- The entry message cites "the fresh 5m BUY". That is **159285**, the 5m candle of 22:30, generated at 22:35:01.553. Its data ends at 22:34:59.999, so it is one full candle older.

**The code** [PROJECT EVIDENCE]:
- **Route:** the entry came through `setupTimeframeAtrAuthoritySignal` (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:1472-1492`), then `setupTimeframeAtrAuthorityDecision` (`:1494-1514`), called at `:628-641`.
- **Which 5m it sees:** the route uses `latestAtOrBefore(current, 5m, FIVE_MINUTE_MAX_AGE = 20 min)`. The as-of anchor is the **1m signal's `generated_at`** (`:2455-2462`), not the decision instant and not the 1m candle close.
  - So whether the 5m candle that closed at the same boundary is visible depends on which analysis thread stamps `generated_at` first.
  - 159407 was stamped after 159408, so it was invisible to the 1m decision.
- **Counterfactual** [AGENT INFERENCE, needs a Replay run]:
  - With 5m = WATCH, `isBullish(five.getDecision())` fails at `:1486` and the route returns null.
  - The 1m signal had `atrImmediateEntryAllowed = false` (`WAIT_FOR_RETRACEMENT`, 0%). Execution would then hit `ATR_ENTRY_BLOCKED` (`:644-649`), so **trade 143 would most likely not have opened**.
- **Scope:** every 1m candle that closes on a 5-minute boundary has this race. For the other five trades:
  - 137: the 5m signal 156721 was stamped 18 ms before the 1m signal, so the current 5m was visible.
  - 158: the 5m signal 165415 was stamped 20 ms after the 1m signal, but it entered through the wakeup path, which correctly consumed it.
  - 162, 172 and 182 do not close on a 5-minute boundary.

**Classification: candidate logic defect.** The higher-timeframe confirmation is admitted on an as-of timestamp that is not deterministic at the candle-close boundary. DBQ-CLAUDE-I4-003 measures how often this happens across all BUYs.

### F4-2: The SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR route bypasses the FIX-154 higher-timeframe assessment and its audit entirely [PROJECT EVIDENCE]

- **Audit rows (I3-001):** five of the six entries have a `buy_htf_assessment_audit` row: 137 and 182 `DIRECT_BUY`, 158 `SETUP_WAKEUP`, 162 `ACCUMULATED_EVIDENCE`, 172 `DIRECT_BUY`. **Signal 159408 (trade 143) has none.**
- **Why:** the audit is written only from `TradeExecutionValidationService.validateBuy` (`:96,111`) or `validateBuyContext` (`:128ff`, via `audit()` `:437-439`), plus consolidation.
  - The SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR route (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:628-641`) calls neither of them. It returns before the direct path at `:652-653`.
  - The FIX-014 SETUP_TIMEFRAME wakeup (`:363`) also skips the validation service.
  - By contrast, the FIX-023 confirmation wakeup does call it (`:435`).
- **Its own 1h check is weaker:** the route rejects only when `isBearish(oneHour.getDecision())` (`:1490`). It does not check `getOriginalDecision()`, data quality, or the FIX-154 bands. Sibling routes do check these (`:337`, `:1425-1426`, `:1552-1553`).
- **Classification: confirmed coverage gap.** This is not a cause of the 143 loss: the 1h was NEUTRAL, so any check would have passed. But it means FIX-154 evidence does not exist for this route, and its gate does not apply. Because of F3-5, FIX-154 is running in Production, so this is an actual Production behaviour.

### F4-3: Trade 182's masking chain is now confirmed end to end [PROJECT EVIDENCE: I3-001, I3-002 + code]

1. **The 1h signal 175258** was generated at 09:00:04.880. It was WATCH with trend 16/25, regime WEAK_UPTREND, and its own confluence UNAVAILABLE.
2. **The 1h counts as bullish context.** `MultiTimeframeConfluenceService.isBullishContext` is `isBullish(decision) || trendScore >= BULLISH_TREND_MINIMUM (15)` (`:24`, `:337-339`). A trend score of 16 qualifies.
3. **Every 5m STRONG_SELL becomes NEUTRAL.** Signals 176278, 176403, 176529, **176655** and 176792 all read `original_decision = STRONG_SELL` → `decision = NEUTRAL`, `CONFLICT`. The explanation reads "Bearish signal conflicts with bullish higher-timeframe structure … 1h=WATCH (trend 16/25)".
4. **The FIX-154 assessment reads the masked value.** It uses `decision()`, which is `getDecision()` (`HigherTimeframeBuyAssessmentService.java:20,50`), so `5M_BEARISH_VETO` (`:22-25`) cannot fire.
   - Audit row 2425 records `five_minute_raw_decision = NEUTRAL` for 176655, even though its original decision was STRONG_SELL.
   - The result was `BALANCED_NEUTRAL_5M_WATCH_1H`, NO_CONFLICT, a 25% intent. The fill was 15% (wallet 451).
5. **The mask lifts at 10:00.** The 1h signal 176791 has trend 13, which is below 15, so the status becomes MIXED. 5m signal 176934 is then an unmasked STRONG_SELL, and the trade exits through SELL_CONFIRMED at 10:05:18.504.

**Two further defects:**
- The audit column labelled "raw" stores the masked value. That defeats the AGENTS.md FIX-154 aim of immutable evidence.
- Note that the 1m signal's own regime was WEAK_DOWNTREND (I2), while the 57-minute-old 1h said WEAK_UPTREND.

**Classification: confirmed logic defect.** Confluence protection meant to stop a bearish 5m from shorting into a bullish 1h is reused as "5m is NEUTRAL" permission to go long. That contradicts the intent of `5M_BEARISH_VETO`. Trade 137 was checked for the same pattern: its 5m signal 156721 had original decision NEUTRAL (I1-004), so it is **not** affected.

### F4-4: Confirmed: an exit transaction freezes price ingestion for every symbol for 0.8–3.2 s [PROJECT EVIDENCE: I3-003 + code]

**Code:**
- `BinanceWebSocketHandler.handleTextMessage` (`:42-67`) calls `service.processKline` synchronously on a single primary session. That call runs `livePositionProtectionService.onPrice` inside `transactions.process` (`BinanceKlineService.java:101-111`).
- So a 1–3 s protection transaction blocks the WebSocket callback thread.

**Data:**

| Exit | Paper close | Events affected | Ingestion lag | Lag before / after |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 137 | 21:00:12.122 | Every event observed 21:00:09.447–12.325 (ICP, AERO, BTC, ETH, BNB, SUI, PEPE, XRP, ENA, SOL, BICO, …) was written at 21:00:12.155–12.341 | Up to **2,708 ms** | −150 ms clock skew just before; it returns to that baseline at 12.577 |
| 143 | 22:43:36.229 | Events observed 35.471–36.317 were written from 36.245 | Up to **774 ms** | 0–23 ms just before and after |

**Impact:**
- During those windows, **no other open position receives a protection evaluation**. The prices are not lost: they are delivered late, in a burst.
- Of the frozen trades, only one position was open at a time on these occasions. So this did not cause any of the six losses [AGENT INFERENCE, based on I1/I2 open/close times].
- It is an architectural risk for a cascade of exits. It also bears on the AGENTS.md "Binance observation timestamp, validate age" requirement: a late burst can be judged against stale-age limits.

### F4-5: Where the 1.2–3.2 s goes inside the transaction [PROJECT EVIDENCE: I3-004 + code; cost attribution is AGENT INFERENCE]

- **Snapshot timing:** `wallet_snapshot.captured_at` falls 2–5 ms **before** the paper close:

  | Snapshot | captured_at | Paper close |
  |---|---|---|
  | 347 | 12.117793 | 12.122 |

  | 402 | 06.637 | 06.638 |
  | 455 | 20.484 | 20.486 |

  `capturedAt(Instant.now())` is stamped **after** `overviewWithoutHistory()` (`WalletService.java:592-599`). So the delay lies between the wallet `executedAt` and the end of the snapshot computation.
- **Query count:** there are 20 `wallet_asset` rows. `currentPortfolioValue()` (`:484-490`) and `overviewWithoutHistory()` (`:600-605`) each loop over all of them, calling `findFirstBySymbolAndIntervalCodeAndClosedTrueOrderByCloseTimeDesc` (`:607`). That is about 40 queries, plus `totalRealizedPnl` and `netInvested` aggregates.
- **Likely cause:** the best index is `idx_candle_dashboard_closed_time (symbol, interval_code, closed, open_time)` (V58). The query orders by **`close_time`**, so MySQL probably sorts all closed 1m rows per symbol rather than reading the index in order. This is a hypothesis; DBQ-CLAUDE-I4-001 tests it with EXPLAIN.
- **Classification:** a performance defect that amplifies F4-4. It did not cause any loss.
