## FINDINGS

### F5-1: The Entry Quality model cannot block an R:R < 1 entry on its own [PROJECT EVIDENCE: code; arithmetic is AGENT INFERENCE]

**How the score works (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:976-1022`):**
- The score starts at 100.
- An R:R below 1 subtracts 30 (`:991`). That leaves 70, which is GOOD_ENTRY.
- The hard block is only a score below 50 (`CHASE_ENTRY_CUTOFF`, `:229`, `:1173`).
- The FIX-091 rejection-zone penalty is clamped at 50 (`:1000-1001`).
- The guard can only *reduce* the size (`:1213-1214`).

**What happened in the frozen set:**
- Five of the six entries had R:R < 1 at the fill price, computed with `currentRewardRisk` (`:1811-1817`).
- All five executed, and three scored exactly 50, which is the inclusive cutoff.

| ID | Fill | R:R at fill | Gross breakeven win rate | Fee-inclusive R:R (0.1%/side) | Fee-inclusive breakeven | Stored entry-quality score |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 137 | 3.999 | 0.64 | 61% | 0.17 | 85% | 58 |
| 143 | 4.033 | **1.39** | 42% | 0.77 | 57% | 70 (message says 60, see F5-3) |
| 158 | 1.4108 | 0.35 | 74% | **< 0**: the TP 0.171% is below the fees | not achievable | 50 |
| 162 | 0.08705 | 0.44 | 70% | 0.03 | 97% | 50 (LATE_ENTRY) |
| 172 | 5.49e-6 | 0.74 nominal, **1.00 at tick resolution** (see F5-4) | 50% | 0.46 | 68% | 60 |
| 182 | 0.1884 | 0.48 | 68% | 0.06 | 94% | 50 |

- **Sources:** the fills are from I1-003, I2-006 and I3-006, the SL/TP from I1-002, and the stored scores from I2-002.
- **Fees:** the fee rate is EXTERNAL RESEARCH from I2. Paper fills record `fee_usdt = 0`.
- **Classification: design gap, not a code defect.** The rules allowed these entries. Combined with I2 F-2 (the 0.5% stop floor makes R:R < 1 whenever ATR is below 0.1667%), the system routinely opens trades with negative expectancy at a reduced 15–25% size. Nothing in the entry-quality score accounts for fees.

### F5-2: What each stored score is made of [AGENT INFERENCE from code constants; I5-001/002 will verify]

**137 = 58.** The only decomposition that fits is 100 − 30 (R:R) − 12 (rejection zone), since the regime was RANGE and so no BREAKOUT or HIGH_VOLATILITY terms apply.
- This means the entry price was within 0.5% of a recent 30-minute high, with at least 2 lower prints after it.
- So a RANGE_MEAN_REVERSION STRONG_BUY bought next to a high that had just been rejected.
- That is a weak buy location known before entry, and the rule floor softened it.

**143.** The R:R of 1.39 carries no penalty, so the remaining penalty of 30–40 must come from expansion, ATR extension or age. Per the code, that is roughly 1.5–3% above the 30-minute reference and 4–6 ATR.
- The model still rated this ACCEPTABLE or GOOD, even though the 1m signal's own ATR policy said `WAIT_FOR_RETRACEMENT` at 0% and RSI was 84 (I2 F-4).
- Entry Quality does not use RSI or distance from SMA20.

**172 = 60**, that is 70 − 10.
- SHIB's ATR (6.8e-9) is smaller than one tick (1e-8), so one tick is about 1.47 ATR.
- Two ticks above the 30-minute low therefore exceed 2.5 ATR and trigger the −10 penalty (`:985`).
- The penalty is a resolution artefact, not a real measure of extension.

**158, 162 and 182 = 50.** After the −30 R:R penalty, each needs another −20. Several combinations of expansion, ATR extension, age and STOP_EXPOSED fit.
- The 162 message confirms LATE_ENTRY 50 (I2-006).
- Which terms applied is UNKNOWN (U18).

### F5-3: Trade 143 had two different Entry Quality scores, computed on different ATR bases, for the same route [PROJECT EVIDENCE: code + I2-006 + I2-002]

- **Decision time:** the SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR route scores Entry Quality with the **5m ATR** (`:634`, "the authoritative 5m ATR so a tiny 1m ATR cannot manufacture a false … chase"). The wallet message shows **60/100 ACCEPTABLE_ENTRY**.
- **Execution time:** both `revalidateAtExecutionPrice` (`:1167`) and the persisted score (`PaperTradingService.java:377-378`) use `assessEntryQualityAtPrice`. That method scores with **`current.getAtrAtSignal()`, the 1m ATR** (`:857-858`). `wallet_managed_position` 172 stores **70**.
- **Consequences:**
  1. The persisted audit score does not match the score that sized the trade.
  2. The execution-time guard uses the ATR basis that the route was written to avoid. When the 1m ATR is tiny, the case the comment describes, the revalidation can turn a decision-approved entry into CHASE_ENTRY_BLOCKED. That is a hidden Production/Replay behaviour difference from the route's stated intent.
  3. Here the 1m score was *higher*, which implies the 1m ATR was larger than the 5m ATR for 159285. If so, the route's premise did not hold in this case (I5-001 will confirm).
- **Classification: confirmed inconsistency in code and data.** It did not cause the 143 loss, because the guard only reduces size and the size stayed 25%.

### F5-4: Trade 172's real geometry at tick resolution [PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE]

- **Nominal levels:** TP 5.5104e-6 and SL 5.46255e-6 around the fill at 5.49e-6.
- **Levels the price can actually trade at:**
  - The TP needs 5.52 (+3 ticks, +0.546%).
  - The SL needs 5.46 (−3 ticks, −0.546%).

- **Classification:** valid loss under the current rules. The design gap is that nothing checks tick size or price resolution (the tick size is assumed from Binance; it is not stored).
