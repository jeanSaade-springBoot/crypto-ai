# Claude: FTS WINDOW2, Iteration 7 (self-challenge)

**Scope.** This covers the six frozen Production trades only (182, 172, 162, 158, 143 and 137 in `crypto_ai.paper_position`). It is analysis only: no code, config, DB, git or deploy changes. I did not read Codex's files, `LIVE_*` or `DATABASE_QUERY_LOG.md`. The plan-file Write and ExitPlanMode tools were not available, so this report is inline, as in Iterations 3–6.

## Bottom line

My two main Iteration 6 conclusions don't hold up against the I6 results.

1. **I withdraw "stop/target geometry against costs" as the root cause of these recorded losses.**
   - These six are **gross** losses: the exit was below the entry, from −0.13% to −0.34% (I6-001). Paper trading charges no fees, so fees caused none of the recorded loss.
   - At population level, the low-ATR, R:R < 0.5 band is the **best gross band**: 9 of 12 won, average +0.072% (I6-002).
   - The money is lost in the high-ATR, R:R ≥ 1.5 band: average −0.150% gross, with 21 stop-outs out of 35.
   - Geometry against costs is still real, but it is a different finding. Net of an assumed 0.2% round trip, every band is negative. That is a profitability warning about the whole system, not the reason these six lost.
2. **Giving back gains is a pattern across the population, not just in these six.**
   - 21 of 47 losers reached 30% or more of the way to their target before closing at a loss (I6-003).
   - Five of the six frozen trades did too: 137 at about 47%, 158 at 33%, 162 at 31%, 172 at 33%, and 143 at about 95% of its extended target. Only 182 never moved in its favour (0%).
3. **Time-zone rule T1 is downgraded from "confirmed" to "supported by the code, but the proof was circular."**
4. **The Iteration 6 guard claims needed corrections:**
   - The guard counterfactual for 182 holds, but for a narrower reason than I gave.
   - The guard does **not** cover 137, which I had implied.
   - My Iteration 6 "score < 60" argument confused two different scores.

## FINDINGS

### F7-1. The fee/geometry root cause is withdrawn for the recorded losses

**PROJECT EVIDENCE: I6-001, I6-002**

The frozen six fall into these bands:

| Band | Frozen trades in the band | Band result |
|---|---|---|
| R:R < 0.5, ATR < 0.10% | 158, 162, 182 | 9 of 12 won gross; the 3 losers are most likely exactly these three |
| R:R 0.5–1, ATR 0.10–0.167% | 137, 172 | 6 of 11 won |
| R:R ≥ 1.5, ATR ≥ 0.25% | 143 | — |

- **What this means (AGENT INFERENCE):** a low R:R did not make losses likely. In this sample, small targets were usually hit, because 47 of 48 winners are in the ≥ 70% progress band.
- **Earlier claim corrected:** in Iteration 6 I wrote that 158 "can't be won net of fees." That is true net of fees, but it is irrelevant to its recorded −0.128% gross loss.
- **The cost finding becomes system-level:**
  - Assuming a 0.2% round trip, the average net result is negative in every band (I6-002: −0.128% to −0.350%).
  - The fee assumption is still unverified (U-I6-2).
  - This changes proposal P3 from "the root cause for 158/162" to "the economics of the whole system, which the zero-fee ledger hides."

### F7-3. Correcting the Iteration 6 guard counterfactual (E5)

**PROJECT EVIDENCE: code and I6-004**

- `balancedEarlyPostBearishGuard` (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:2078-2137`) arms only on a **5m** SELL/STRONG_SELL within 20 minutes (`:178`, `:2090-2095`). It reads `strongerDecision(decision, original_decision)`. A run of **1m** STRONG_SELL signals does not arm it.
- **For 182:**
  - Its 5m signals 176278, 176403, 176529 and 176655 all show `decision` NEUTRAL with `original_decision` STRONG_SELL. The last of them was generated at 09:55:03 shown time, before the 09:57:06 entry.
  - So the guard would arm, and the recovery count would be 0 against the required 2 (`:2097-2107`). That alone would pause the entry.
  - **Withdrawn:** my Iteration 6 "score 50 < 60" argument. The 60 at `:181`/`:2108` is the Entry Quality score, not `total_score`. I have not verified 182's Entry Quality.
- **For 137:**
  - Every 5m signal in its 20-minute lookback has NEUTRAL for both `decision` and `original_decision` (156216 to 156721).
  - So the guard would **not** arm, and P2 would not change 137. The nine-signal 1m STRONG_SELL run from E6 is not read by this guard.
- **New point for 182 (PROJECT EVIDENCE, `AnalysisService.java:364-411`):**
  - `original_decision` is the base decision from the score. `decision` is what FinalDecisionService returns.
  - The FIX-11G route, BALANCED_NEUTRAL_5M_WATCH_1H, therefore treated a 5m bar demoted from STRONG_SELL to NEUTRAL as genuinely neutral, with 5m scores of 22–28.
  - Why the demotion happened is unknown (I7-003).

### F7-4. Time-zone rule T1: my earlier proof was circular

**PROJECT EVIDENCE plus a self-correction**

- **Why I3-002 proves nothing:** `UNIX_TIMESTAMP(col)` interprets the displayed value in the session zone. The session is SYSTEM, which is Arab Standard Time, +180 minutes (I3-001). So the query returns "shown minus 3 h" for **any** column type.
- **What still supports T1:**
  - The app writes with session `+00:00` (`application.yml:22`, `:24`, `:35`).
  - The baseline DDL declares these columns `TIMESTAMP(6)` (`B105__fix_153_trader_only_baseline.sql:80`, `:151`, `:187-188`, `:215`).
  - MySQL converts `TIMESTAMP` values to the session zone when reading them. So *if* the Production columns really are `TIMESTAMP`, the wrapper shows KSA and T1 holds.
  - Not every column is `TIMESTAMP`: `btc_signal_generated_at` is `DATETIME(6)` (`:509`).
- **Status:** T1 is plausible but not proven until I7-001 confirms the actual Production column types. Until then:
  - Each time I quote is labelled "shown," meaning the wrapper display.
  - The KSA reading of it follows T1.
  - The UTC reading is shown time minus 3 h, but **only if T1 holds**.
- **Example:** 182 was entered at 09:57:06 shown, which is 09:57:06 KSA and 06:57:06 UTC under T1.
- If I7-001 shows `DATETIME`, every KSA time in Iterations 3–6 shifts +3 h. That would not change any difference between two times in this study.
