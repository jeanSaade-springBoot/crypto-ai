## FINDINGS

### F6-1. Shared root cause for 137, 158, 162, 172 and 182: low-edge entries closed by a bearish exit signal (PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE)

**How these losses happen (PROJECT EVIDENCE):**
1. The entry used one of four routes (IMMEDIATE_VALIDATION, SETUP_CONFIRMATION_WAKEUP, ACCUMULATED_EVIDENCE, SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR). None of them has an R:R floor (iteration-4 R1).
2. The entry-quality score blocks only below 50 (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:1173`).
3. The signal's R:R was 0.35–0.74, and the target was only 0.17–0.37% away.
4. Price went at most 31–47% of the way to target (182 never went above its entry). That is below the 70% level where the profit lock arms, so the lock never armed.
5. The exit was then decided by `PositionExitPolicy.evaluateNormalExit`: `SELL_CONFIRMED` (`:35-43`) or `HTF_SELL_CONFIRMED` (`:25-33`).

**System-wide (PROJECT EVIDENCE, I5-003):** these signal exits had **0 wins out of 23** in October, for a total of about −1.27. The policy itself does not require the position to be in loss (`:20-50`).

**AGENT INFERENCE:** winning positions usually arm the profit lock first and leave through the `PROFIT_LOCK*` exits. A signal exit therefore mostly catches trades that never worked. The six frozen trades are what you would expect from that group: small losses of −0.13% to −0.34% of the wallet.

**Classification:** each trade was a valid losing trade under the coded rules. The root cause is an **entry-selection policy** that admits setups where even reaching the target barely pays. It is not a logic defect, stale data or a delayed execution.

### F6-2. 162 cleared the cutoff by 30 ms of opportunity age, and was score 50 by exact arithmetic (PROJECT EVIDENCE)

Working through the scoring code at `:976-1022` with the stored values:
- **R:R:** 0.44 gives −30.
- **ATR extension:** measured from the opportunity's best price 0.08675 to the 0.08705 fill, with an ATR of 0.0000636. That is 4.72 ATR, which gives −20.
- **Price expansion:** 0.35%, so no penalty.
- **BREAKOUT bonus:** not given, because the extension is above 2.5.
- **Rejection zone:** none. The message has no rejection text (`:1208-1211`).
- **Result:** 100 − 30 − 20 = **50**.

**Opportunity age:** the signal was generated at 1791766922.813 and the opportunity started at 1791765662.837. The gap is **1259.976 s**. `toMinutes()` truncates that to 20, and the penalty applies only above 20 (`:987-989`), so no age penalty was taken.

**What 30 ms changed:** had the gap been 30 ms longer, the score would have been 45. That is CHASE_ENTRY_BLOCKED, and **162 would not have been opened**.

**FIX-119:** it was bypassed because `buy_count=1` (`:1275-1280`). One BUY among 20 evidence observations is enough to lift the deferral.

**FIX-041:** it does not apply. That block is only on the direct route (`:672`), and the comment at `:668-671` deliberately leaves accumulated evidence out of it.

**Verdict:** the trade followed the design, and the outcome sat right on two boundaries. **AGENT INFERENCE:** this is a policy-sensitivity finding, not a defect.

### F6-3. 143: the stored score does not match the score that sized the trade (PROJECT EVIDENCE + UNKNOWN)

**Where each score comes from:**
- **The decision-time score was 60.** It was computed with the 5m ATR (`:634`) and is the score that set the 25% cap.
- **The stored score is 70.** `PaperTradingService.java:377-378` computes it again at the fill price with the **1m** ATR (`:857-858`) and stores it on the position.
- **Effect on sizing:** none. At the fill, cap = min(25, 40) = 25 (`:1183,1213`).

**Why the direction looks wrong:**
- On R:R alone, the fill re-score should be lower than the decision score. At the 4.032 decision price, R:R was 1.50, which gets no penalty. At the 4.033 fill, it was 1.39, which gets −15.
- A smaller ATR should also make the fill-time score lower.
- Instead it was higher, so something else changed between the decision and the fill: the opportunity's reference price, its age, or the rolling window. The likely candidate is the `saveOpportunity` update at `:636`. This cause is **UNKNOWN (U-I6-1)**.

**Audit impact:** `entry_quality_score` on the position does not reliably record the decision that was made. This is an evidence-integrity gap, not a cause of the loss.

**Correction to iteration-5 E4:** E4 predicted that re-scoring with the 1m ATR would *downgrade* a valid 5m setup. On 143 the observed re-score went *up*. The inconsistency in the code still stands, but the direction of its effect is not proven.

### F6-4. Decomposing 137's score (PROJECT EVIDENCE)

- 58 = 100 − 30 (R:R 0.64) − 12.
- The other penalties are all multiples of 5, plus 22. The only one that can make −12 is `RECENT_REJECTION_ENTRY_QUALITY_PENALTY` (`:236`).
- The RANGE regime rules out the +5 BREAKOUT bonus that could otherwise offset a −5.
- **So 137 entered within 0.5% of a recently rejected high**, with no extension, expansion or age penalty. The iteration-5 inference is now proven by the arithmetic.

### F6-5. 182 and 158 cannot be decomposed yet (PROJECT EVIDENCE + UNKNOWN)

**182:**
- Measured from the anchor 0.1879, the extension is 3.31 ATR (ATR 0.000151). That gives −10, so the score should have been 60, not 50.
- One of two things must be true. Either the rolling 30-minute low was ≤ 0.18780, which makes the extension above 4 (−20). Or a rejection zone took 60 − 12 = 48, which the code raises back to the floor of 50 (`:1000-1002`).
- 182 also entered with **bearish_count=1** in its evidence and a WEAK_DOWNTREND regime.

**158:** the opportunity row was truncated out of I5-002. → I6-001/002.

### F6-6. 143's distinct cause (PROJECT EVIDENCE, carried and now closed)

The entry was on the SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR route:
- The 1m signal was WATCH with RSI 84.3 and `rsi_score` 1/15.
- The 1m ATR plan said 0%, which the code treats as 100% (iteration-5 E5).
- It re-entered 22 s after trade 142 exited, and nothing on the route checks for a cooldown (iteration-4 R5).
- The profit lock armed, then a price gap went straight through it.

**Root cause:** a valid gap loss after a stretched entry. Contributing gaps are no momentum input to entry quality (E3) and no re-entry cooldown.

### F6-8. Fees (EXTERNAL RESEARCH + PROJECT EVIDENCE)

**External:**
- Several secondary sources agree that Binance global spot VIP-0 is 0.1% maker and 0.1% taker. That makes a round trip about 0.2%, or about 0.15% when paying with BNB.
- I did not confirm this on Binance's own fee page, so treat it as moderately reliable.
- Binance.US fees are different and do not apply here.

**Project:** a grep of `src/main/java` and `application.yml` found no fee or commission model on the paper path. This was a limited search.

**AGENT INFERENCE:**
- 158's 0.17% target is below the round-trip fee, and 172's target was about one price tick.
- Even a target hit would have been flat or negative live.
- Paper P&L probably understates live losses on every trade.

### F6-9. Can it be reproduced in Replay? (PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE)

- Entry quality, `revalidateAtExecutionPrice`, the confluence logic and `PositionExitPolicy` are all shared with Replay (`ShadowProductionReplayService.java:300,335`; the comment at `:671`; the policy javadoc at `:8`).
- Scores depend on persisted signal prices and on the opportunity state measured from candle time (`:894-897`).
- So F6-1, F6-2 and F6-4 should reproduce, as long as Replay rebuilds the same `execution_opportunity` state.
- F6-3 depends on how that opportunity state changes between the decision and the fill. Replay can reproduce it only if it applies the same `saveOpportunity` sequence. I have not verified that.
- 162's 30 ms age margin makes its Replay result **fragile**. Any difference of more than 30 ms in Replay's `generatedAt` or `startedAt` flips it to blocked.
