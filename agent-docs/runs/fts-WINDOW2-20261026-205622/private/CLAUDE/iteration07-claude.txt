## FINDINGS

### F-42 — Where the system loses money: the stop-loss class, plus slippage past the stop (PROJECT EVIDENCE: I6-001 and I6-002; the arithmetic is AGENT INFERENCE)

- **The I6-001 groups reconcile exactly:** they sum to −4.1707 USDT across 193 positions.
- **Totals:**
  - `CLOSED` positions: **+11.70 USDT**.
  - `STOPPED` positions: **−15.88 USDT** across 75, all losses.
  - Stop-loss exits are about 92% of all gross losses. Signal-exit losers are only about −1.4 USDT.
- **Payoff shape (AGENT INFERENCE):**
  - Average win is about +0.44%; average loss is about −0.50%.
  - With a 47% win rate, break-even would need about 53% wins.
  - 69 of the 90 wins are profit-lock exits. They realise only 0.27% and 0.47%, against average targets of 0.65% and 1.20%.
- **Stops fill worse than the stop price:**
  - Average loss is 1.151 times the stop distance; 3 of 75 exceed 1.5 times, and the worst is 2.27 times (trade 151, PEPE).
  - Average loss is 0.600% against an average stop distance of 0.525%. That is about 0.075% × 2,710 notional ≈ **2.0 USDT**, roughly half of the system's gross loss.
- **PEPE dominates the overshoot list** (6 of the top 10). My hypothesis is the coarse price step on a very low-priced coin combined with fast drops, like trade 143's sweep (F-11). This is unproven; I7-003 tests it.
- **Caveat:** `paper_position.stop_loss` may be the stop after any later adjustment, not the stop set at entry.
- **What this means for the study:** the frozen six are the *cheapest* kind of loser. Their signal exits lost 0.18–0.26% on average versus 0.60% for stops, and together they lost −0.273 USDT (about 6.5% of the gross loss). The biggest improvement opportunity is outside this frozen selection.

### F-39 — Holds for trade 137; rejected as a population-wide or trade-182 cause (PROJECT EVIDENCE: I6-003 and code; counterfactuals are AGENT INFERENCE)

- **Sentiment is a time-of-day feed, not random gaps.** It is available only from about 03:00 to 08:00 UTC (06:00–11:00 KSA) each day, and on no other hours in 10-11 to 10-15.
  - Trade 137 entered at 20:55 UTC, when sentiment is structurally absent.
  - So "with sentiment" versus "without" is confounded with time of day.
- **Population result (1m entries; every position entered on a 1m signal):**

  | Sentiment at entry | Positions | Wins | P&L (USDT) | P&L per dollar traded | Average score |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Absent | 142 | 66 | −2.517 | −0.054% | 72.4 |
  | Present | 43 | 20 | −1.628 | **−0.124%** | 70.9 |

  - Positions with sentiment did *worse*. The claim "missing data inflates scores and leads to worse trades" is not supported across the population.
- **Trade 137 is robust.** It used the RANGE profile, where sentiment is worth up to 15 points.
  - With the observed feed value: (50 + 10) / 70 = 86, a BUY rather than STRONG_BUY.
  - With a neutral value instead: (50 + 7.5) / 70 = 82.
  - Both are below 88 (STRONG_BUY) and below the 90 needed for the range exception, so the range-location guard would have blocked the entry.
- **New self-challenge on trade 182 — tested and rejected.** The trend-following profile allows sentiment up to 10 points (`application.yml:353`). Scaling is rounded (`MarketStrategyService.java:211-215`).
  - With the observed feed value: (49 + 7) / 75 = 74.67, which rounds to 75, still a BUY at the 75 threshold. Leaving sentiment out did **not** decide trade 182.
  - Only a neutral value of 5 points would have flipped it, to 72 (WATCH).

SELECT p.id, p.symbol, p.close_reason, p.closed_at, p.entry_price, p.exit_price, p.take_profit, p.stop_loss, COUNT(c.id) AS n_candles, MAX(c.high_price) AS max_high_120m, MIN(c.low_price) AS min_low_120m, MIN(CASE WHEN c.high_price >= p.take_profit THEN c.open_time END) AS first_tp_touch, MIN(CASE WHEN c.low_price <= p.stop_loss THEN c.open_time END) AS first_sl_touch FROM crypto_ai.paper_position p LEFT JOIN crypto_ai.candle c ON c.symbol = p.symbol AND c.interval_code = '1m' AND c.closed = 1 AND c.open_time >= p.closed_at AND c.open_time < DATE_ADD(p.closed_at, INTERVAL 120 MINUTE) WHERE p.id IN (182,172,162,158,143,137) GROUP BY p.id, p.symbol, p.close_reason, p.closed_at, p.entry_price, p.exit_price, p.take_profit, p.stop_loss ORDER BY p.id LIMIT 6;

### DB_REQUEST
QueryId: DBQ-CLAUDE-I7-002
Purpose: Outcome only. For every `CLOSED` exit other than `TAKE_PROFIT`: after the exit, which was touched first within 120 minutes, the target or the stop? (U-35; tests whether profit locks help or hurt.)
SQL:
SELECT x.close_reason, COUNT(*) AS n, SUM(x.first_sl IS NOT NULL AND (x.first_tp IS NULL OR x.first_sl < x.first_tp)) AS sl_first, SUM(x.first_tp IS NOT NULL AND (x.first_sl IS NULL OR x.first_tp < x.first_sl)) AS tp_first, SUM(x.first_tp IS NOT NULL AND x.first_tp = x.first_sl) AS same_candle, SUM(x.first_tp IS NULL AND x.first_sl IS NULL) AS neither, ROUND(SUM(x.realized_pnl), 4) AS pnl FROM (SELECT p.id, p.close_reason, p.realized_pnl, MIN(CASE WHEN c.high_price >= p.take_profit THEN c.open_time END) AS first_tp, MIN(CASE WHEN c.low_price <= p.stop_loss THEN c.open_time END) AS first_sl FROM crypto_ai.paper_position p LEFT JOIN crypto_ai.candle c ON c.symbol = p.symbol AND c.interval_code = '1m' AND c.closed = 1 AND c.open_time >= p.closed_at AND c.open_time < DATE_ADD(p.closed_at, INTERVAL 120 MINUTE) WHERE p.status = 'CLOSED' AND p.close_reason <> 'TAKE_PROFIT' GROUP BY p.id, p.close_reason, p.realized_pnl) x GROUP BY x.close_reason ORDER BY n DESC LIMIT 20;
### END_DB_REQUEST

### DB_REQUEST
QueryId: DBQ-CLAUDE-I7-003
Purpose: Stop overshoot cost per symbol, to test the price-step hypothesis (F-42, U-36).
SQL:
SELECT p.symbol, COUNT(*) AS n, ROUND(AVG((p.entry_price - p.exit_price) / (p.entry_price - p.stop_loss)), 3) AS avg_loss_to_stop, ROUND(SUM((p.exit_price - p.stop_loss) * p.quantity), 4) AS overshoot_usdt, ROUND(SUM(p.realized_pnl), 4) AS pnl, ROUND(SUM(p.entry_price * p.quantity), 2) AS notional, MIN(p.stop_loss) AS min_stop FROM crypto_ai.paper_position p WHERE p.status = 'STOPPED' AND p.entry_price > p.stop_loss GROUP BY p.symbol ORDER BY overshoot_usdt LIMIT 30;
### END_DB_REQUEST
