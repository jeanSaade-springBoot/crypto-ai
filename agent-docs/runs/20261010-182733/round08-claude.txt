### B. New findings

**N46 (material). §3's "permitted evidence" includes Production account state.**
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** `signal_processing_work` has a `paper_position_id` column and records Production's execution outcome.
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** `execution_opportunity` stores `executed_at` (set when Production executed) and `recommended_position_percent`. Its rows are updated in place.
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** Production `fix122_evaluation` rows come from the wallet ledger:
  - Production takes its stop boundary from `StopLossEvidenceStore.latest`, which queries `wallet_trade`.
  - The stored payload includes the position percent.
  - Replay uses run-local boundaries instead.
- **[AGENT INFERENCE]** §1 forbids reading Production account state, and §3 forbids `wallet_trade` and `paper_position`. Yet §3 permits these three derived tables without saying what they may be used for.
  - Used as simulation inputs (for example, §7's "skipped work is reproduced from durable outcome evidence"), they would import Production fills, position links and stop history into a virtual-account run.
  - That is exactly the account evidence §1 cites as the reason `ENTRY_DECISION_PARITY` is out of scope.

**N45 (moderate). M05 names no enforcement mechanism, and the obvious candidate doesn't cover reads.**
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** FIX-125 already has a repository guard (`WalletMutationRepositoryGuard`) and a source-scan test. Their coverage is narrow:
  - The guard only covers writes and locking reads on six wallet repositories.
  - Plain reads such as `TradeExecutionValidationService.settings()` pass through.
  - The `paper_position` and `wallet_managed_position` repositories aren't guarded.
  - The source scan only matches `UPDATE`, `INSERT` and `DELETE` statements.
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** `StopLossEvidenceService` already switches its boundary and clock between Replay and Production, which is a usable precedent.
- **[AGENT INFERENCE]** The revision should register a concrete guard that rejects any account-table access while Replay is active, plus a source-scan test. It must also say how the guard reaches work handed to other threads (objection 10).

**N47 (moderate). Under processing delay, Production evaluates one fewer signal than Replay.**
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** The two sides fetch signals differently:
  - Production's `recentSignals` takes the 20 newest signals regardless of the reference time.
  - Replay takes the 20 newest signals as of the reference time.
  - All ten callers drop signals newer than the current one, so there is no look-ahead.
- **[PROJECT EVIDENCE]** The 30-minute evidence and deferred-BUY windows span about 30 one-minute signals, so the cap of 20 already applies. 1m work may run up to 90 s after its signal.
- **[AGENT INFERENCE]** When Production processes a 1m signal after the next one exists, opportunity health and deferred-BUY rules see 19 signals; Replay sees 20.
  - The effect is small, but it shows that "same pure policy" isn't enough: the data adapters differ.
  - The spec should declare this as a zero-latency assumption, or add an X06 test case.
