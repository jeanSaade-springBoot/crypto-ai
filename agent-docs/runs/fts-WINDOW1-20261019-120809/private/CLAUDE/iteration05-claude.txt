- **Three of the six were "late" entries at the 50-point cut-off: 158, 162 and 182.** Two guards against late entries exist, but each covers only one narrow route, and these trades came through routes neither guard covers.

## FINDINGS

**F-I5-01: How the entry-quality score works, and why it barely discriminates here (PROJECT EVIDENCE).**
- `ExecutionIntelligenceService.assessEntryQuality` (865–1030) starts at 100 and subtracts for:
  - price expansion above the lowest 1m signal price of the last 30 minutes (or a lower opportunity anchor): −10 / −22 / −35 / −50;
  - extension measured in ATRs: −10 / −20 / −30;
  - age of the opportunity: −5 / −10 / −15;
  - **risk/reward below 1: a flat −30** (line 991);
  - a recently rejected high: −12, but never pushed below 50 (lines 995–1003);
  - STOP_EXPOSED liquidity: −15; HIGH_VOLATILITY regime: −10.
- The BREAKOUT regime gets +5 when the entry is not extended.
- It does **not** look at RSI, Bollinger position, the SMA20 distance, confluence CONFLICT, or whether the regime points against the trade.
- **Agent inference:** every low-ATR entry hits the 0.5% stop floor, which gives risk/reward below 1 (F-I2-04) and an automatic 70 ceiling. A single further −10 or −20 penalty then lands the trade at 60 or 50, which still passes the cut-off of 50 (line 1173).

**F-I5-03: Neither of the two late-entry guards covers the routes these trades used (PROJECT EVIDENCE).**
- **FIX-041** (lines 672–684) blocks a LATE_ENTRY only when the validation code is `BALANCED_EARLY`.
- **FIX-119** (lines 1257–1280) defers an ACCUMULATED_EVIDENCE LATE_ENTRY only when the evidence contains **zero** BUY signals and the 5m is not bullish.
- How each late trade got through:
  - **182** came through IMMEDIATE_VALIDATION with code `BALANCED_NEUTRAL_5M_WATCH_1H` (FIX-11G), so FIX-041 did not apply.
  - **162** had evidence of "1 BUY, 9 WATCH" (I1-002). `buyCount=1 > 0`, so FIX-119 was bypassed by a single BUY out of ten.
  - **158** came through SETUP_CONFIRMATION_WAKEUP (lines 397–456), which only applies the general score ≥ 50 guard.
- **Agent inference:** the rules were followed as written. The coverage gap is a design or config issue, not a code defect.

**F-I5-04: The two "5m neutral" exceptions are inconsistent. The one with weaker higher-timeframe support has the looser gate (PROJECT EVIDENCE: `TradeExecutionValidationService.java:218–298`).**

| Exception | Requires | Confidence | Entry quality | Frozen trades |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FIX-112B (`BALANCED_NEUTRAL_5M`) | 1h **bullish** | ≥ 72 | **≥ 70** | none |
| FIX-11G (`BALANCED_NEUTRAL_5M_WATCH_1H`) | 1h only **WATCH** | no check (line 256–257) | **≥ 50** (line 49) | 137 (confidence 81, quality 58) and 182 (confidence 68, quality 50) |

- Neither 137 nor 182 would have passed the FIX-112B thresholds, even though FIX-112B demands *stronger* 1h support.
- The comment at 256–257 shows the missing confidence check is deliberate.
- **Agent inference:** this is a design inconsistency to raise with Jean, not a bug. It accounts for 2 of the 6 frozen losses.

**F-I5-05: Trade 182 was a counter-regime BUY under a "trend-following" label (PROJECT EVIDENCE).**
- `MarketStrategyService.java:70–72` maps `WEAK_DOWNTREND` and `STRONG_DOWNTREND` to TREND_FOLLOWING without any direction check.
- A search for `DOWNTREND` across `src/main/java/com/crypto` finds only the regime enum, the regime classifier and that strategy mapping. **No check vetoes a BUY in a downtrend regime.**
- What was known about 182 at entry (I1-002/003):
  - 1m regime WEAK_DOWNTREND, trend score 12/25;
  - confluence CONFLICT; 5m NEUTRAL, 1h WATCH;
  - price 2.15 ATR above SMA20 (`atr_overextended=1`, REDUCED_POSITION);
  - confidence 68, quality 50;
  - "No persistent bid support was detected above the stop-loss."
- **Outcome only (hindsight):** the highest price while held was 0.1884, the entry price itself (I1-005). It never went into profit.
- **Classification:** weakest entry of the set; it followed the rules; a design gap.

**F-I5-06: For several trades the entry geometry ruled out a profit before the market moved (PROJECT EVIDENCE plus EXTERNAL fee research from F-I4-09).**

- **Agent inference:** nothing at entry checks the target's distance against fees or tick size, or checks for a wall just in front of the target.

**F-I5-08: Self-review. F-I4-06's explanation of 143's 60 vs 70 is inconsistent, and I downgrade it to UNKNOWN (PROJECT EVIDENCE plus AGENT INFERENCE).**
- The route scored the trade at the signal price 4.032 with the 5m ATR, giving 60.
- The fill check at 349 and the stored value at 378 both use `assessEntryQualityAtPrice` at 4.033, with the same signal and before `markExecuted` (line 401). That gave 70.
- **Problem 1:** the fill price makes risk/reward worse (1.5 → 1.39, costing −15). A larger 5m ATR can only *reduce* the ATR-extension penalty, and makes the +5 BREAKOUT bonus more likely.
- **Problem 2:** if the inputs really were the same, the route score should be 15 or more points *higher* than the stored score, not 10 lower. I could not find any combination of 5m ATR penalties that produces 60.
- **Remaining explanations:**
  - (a) the 5m `atr_at_signal` is smaller than the 1m ATR, through a units or field defect;
  - (b) the reference price or opportunity age seen by the route differed from what execution saw.
- I5-001, I5-002 and I5-003 settle it.
- F-I4-06's claim that the difference "cannot block an entry that the 1m ATR would accept" is withdrawn until then.

### DB_REQUEST
