## FINDINGS

**F-I4-01: The times in this run's database results are KSA, not UTC. This is a correction to Iterations 1–3.**
- **Project evidence:**
  - The timestamp columns are `TIMESTAMP(6)` (for example `B105__fix_153_trader_only_baseline.sql:21,80,188,656`). MySQL converts these to the session's time zone when it displays them.
  - The application writes them in UTC (`application.yml:22`, `SET time_zone = '+00:00'`). So the stored values are correct.
  - The study's query session uses `SYSTEM`, which is Arab Standard Time (I3-007: `now_session` 12:57:28 vs `now_utc` 09:57:28). So it displays every value in KSA.
- **External research (Binance public klines):**
  - The candles stored as "EGLD 22:41, 22:42, 22:43" match Binance's 19:41, 19:42 and 19:43 UTC candles exactly: same open, high, low, close, volume and trade counts (302, 86 and 433 trades).
  - The XRP candle stored as "03:30" matches Binance 00:30 UTC (1.4089 / 1.4090 / 1.4078 / 1.4084, 244 trades).
  - At the "22:43 UTC" I had assumed, the real EGLD price was about 3.97 with only 12 trades, so that reading is wrong.
- **Impact:**
  - Every time labelled "UTC" in my I1–I3 reports is KSA. The real UTC is that value minus 3 hours.
  - Durations, the order of events, and the 1m/5m/1h candle boundaries are unaffected, because KSA is a whole-hour offset with no daylight saving.
  - Query literals I copied from earlier results are read in the same session time zone, so the I1–I3 results stay internally consistent.
  - Exception: `trade_signal.btc_context_evaluated_at` and `btc_signal_generated_at` are `DATETIME`, so they display true UTC. They must never be compared directly with the other timestamps.
- **Contradiction to report:** `DATABASE_INVESTIGATION_RULES.md:35` says "Raw MySQL timestamps are UTC". That is only true for a session set to `+00:00`, and this harness's session is not. I flag it rather than rely on it, and recommend a dual-agent review.

**F-I4-02: Trade 143's 4.100 → 4.027 move was a real market sweep, not lost updates. It is a valid loss with a known design limitation.**
- **External research (Binance EGLDUSDT trade history, 19:43:05–19:43:36 UTC):**
  - Trades happened at :18.643–:19.646 and at :26.041 (4.100).
  - Then there were no trades until :34.341, a gap of 8.3 s.
  - From :34.341 to :35.0 a burst of 120 trades took the price from 4.097 to 4.025. One sell at :34.816 alone moved it from 4.094 to 4.073.
- **Project evidence:**
  - Each stored price update (I3-001) comes 0.4–1.0 s after a burst of trades: :19.021, :21.033, :27.038 and :35.021 (4.027).
  - Every 1m kline message is stored (`BinanceKlineService.java:95,106`). Only invalid or stale prices are skipped (`MarketPriceEventService.java:53-57`).
  - The lag between observation and storage stayed at 7–12 ms across the gap (I3-002, buckets 26–32).
- **Conclusion:**
  - The price sat between the lock (4.0758) and the hard floor (4.0350) only between :34.816 and about :34.88.
  - Binance's kline stream only sends a ~2 s snapshot after trades, so the system could not have seen those prices.
  - The system sold 25 ms after the first price it could see.
  - This follows `LivePositionProtectionService` as designed. The limitation is that paper protection samples the price instead of holding a resting stop order.
- **Outcome only (hindsight):** the 19:44 UTC candle closed at 4.058.

**F-I4-03: The gaps after candle close were quiet markets, not a stalled feed. This refutes the F-I3-03 inference.**
- **Project evidence:**
  - In every gap window, other symbols kept being stored with low lag (I3-002): P172 buckets 2–42 at 11–17 ms, and P182 buckets 2–16 at 33–63 ms.
  - The gap candles had very few trades (I3-004): EGLD 6 trades, SHIB **1** trade, ACE 10 trades. The XRP candle had 244 trades.
- **Inference:** Binance only sends a kline update after a trade. That matches the EGLD trade-by-trade evidence above.
- **Consequence for F-I3-02:**
  - The 6–39 s delay before signal-driven exits happened because no trades occurred. With nothing trading, the last-trade price didn't change, so paper P&L couldn't change.
  - I downgrade this from a latency defect to a design note: an exit decision waits for the next trade, and that wait has no upper limit.

**F-I4-05: How the entries got through. None of the four routes used has a minimum risk/reward. This answers U-I2-05.**
- **Project evidence, `ExecutionIntelligenceService.java`:**

- The final check at the execution price (`revalidateAtExecutionPrice`, 1150-1168) also only rejects a price at or below the stop, or at or above the target.
- **Recorded entry quality scores:** 137 = 58, 162 = 50, 172 = 60, 182 = 50, 143 = 60. Two trades sat exactly at the cut-off.
- **Inference:** the design lets entries in the risk/reward 0.33–0.74 range through by intent: the penalty is soft, not a hard veto. The cause is a config/design gap, not a code bug. This confirms F-I2-04 in the code.

**F-I4-06: Why 143 shows entry quality 70 and 60. This answers U-I2-06.**

**F-I4-07: A signal-path exit uses the candle-close price, not the latest market price (trade 162).**
- **Project evidence:**
  - `closeFromSignal` sells at `signal.getLatestPrice()` (`PaperTradingService.java:449`, called from 207). The SELL branch (197-213) doesn't require a current price.
  - Entries do use a fresh price (`PaperTradingService.java:342-350`, FIX-056).
  - For 162, the exit price 0.08675 was the 01:25 UTC candle close. The latest live price was 0.08673 at 01:26:02.583 UTC (I3-001), and the sale happened about 1.5 s later.
- **Impact here:** the paper exit was 1 tick better than the market (realised −0.0517; at 0.08673 it would be about 0.3 ticks worse per unit). This makes paper results less realistic, and the bias can go either way.
- **Classification:** a minor logic inconsistency. It did not cause the loss.

**F-I4-08: Replay and Production should produce the same P&L for all six trades. Exit time differs only for 158.**
- **Project evidence:** I3-005 shows commit lags (from `generated_at` to `created_at`) of 1288–2278 ms. Replay makes a signal visible at `generatedAt` (`ShadowProductionReplayService.java:127-133`).
- **For 158:**
  - The 5m SELL was generated at 00:30:01.464 UTC and committed at 02.811.
  - A price of 1.409 arrived at 02.320, between those two times.
  - Replay would exit on that 02.320 price; Production exited at 04.319, at the same price.
- **For 137, 172 and 182,** the next price after commit is the same in Replay and Production.
- **For 143** (price only) and **162** (signal close price), Replay should match.
- **Inference:** P&L should match for all six; the exit timestamp should differ by about 2 s for 158. Replay has not been run (U-I3-06).

**F-I4-09: The fee check is done. This closes U-I2-07.**
- **External research:** Binance VIP0 spot fee is 0.100% maker / 0.100% taker, or 0.075% if paid in BNB.
- **Project evidence:** `fee_usdt=0` on the fills, so stored P&L is gross. F-I2-05 still stands: at 0.2% round trip, 158's take-profit (+0.164%) loses money after fees, and 162 and 182 are near zero.

**F-I4-10: The server clock's offset from Binance changes over time. This is a measurement caveat that refines F-I3-06.**

- **Conclusion:** cross-clock comparisons are only reliable to about ±200 ms. This is not a cause of any loss.
