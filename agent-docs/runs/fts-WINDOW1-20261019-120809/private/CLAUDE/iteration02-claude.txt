**F-I2-04 — CONFIRMED STRUCTURAL ISSUE: when volatility is low, the planned target is closer than the stop (PROJECT EVIDENCE: code + DB).**
- In `AtrRiskService.java:40-45`, the stop distance is `max(2·ATR, 0.5%·price)` (`application.yml:143` `minimum-stop-percent: 0.50`). The reward is always `3·ATR` and is never scaled up when the 0.5% floor applies.
- So whenever ATR is below 0.1667% of price, the risk/reward ratio drops below 1.
- I checked this against the DB to the last digit for every trade:

| Pos | ATR% | Stop | TP distance | R/R |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 137 | 0.107% | 0.5% floor | 0.322% | 0.644 |
| 158 | 0.055% | 0.5% floor | 0.164% | 0.327 |
| 162 | 0.073% | 0.5% floor | 0.219% | 0.438 |
| 172 | 0.124% | 0.5% floor | 0.372% | 0.744 |
| 182 | 0.080% | 0.5% floor | 0.241% | 0.482 |
| 143 | 0.270% | 2·ATR = 0.541% | 0.811% | 1.50 |

  Only 143 used the 2·ATR stop.
- Some Execution Intelligence routes enforce a minimum R/R of 1.15–1.25 (`ExecutionIntelligenceService.java:1055,1098,1128,1539,1631`).
- The routes these trades actually used are IMMEDIATE_VALIDATION, SETUP_CONFIRMATION_WAKEUP, ACCUMULATED_EVIDENCE and SETUP_TIMEFRAME_ATR. They admitted R/R as low as 0.33. I have not yet traced those routes line by line.

**F-I2-05 — With fees, the targets are at or below break-even (AGENT INFERENCE, based on EXTERNAL RESEARCH that I have not re-verified).**
- The recorded fee is `fee_usdt=0` on all 12 fills (I1-006), so the stored P&L is gross.
- I assumed the Binance spot standard fee of 0.1% per side, which is 0.2% round trip.
  - 158's take-profit (+0.164%) would be a **net loss**.
  - 162 (+0.019% net) and 182 (+0.041% net) are close to zero.
  - Over the 130 USDT traded, the assumed fees (≈0.26 USDT) are about the same as the gross losses (0.273 USDT).
- This supports a likely configuration/design defect: on 1m charts in low volatility, the trades have negative expectancy before any entry signal is considered.

**F-I2-07 — 143 gave back most of a winning trade: a defect candidate (PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE).**
- Timeline (UTC → KSA):
  - Entry 4.033.
  - The profit lock activated at 22:40:23 → 01:40:23.
  - The TP was extended to 4.0805 at 22:40:41 → 01:40:41, then to 4.1043 at 22:41:13 → 01:41:13 (I1-007).
  - The peak was 4.101, which is +1.69% and 95.35% of the way to the extended TP.
  - The final lock state is `ACTIVE` with the lock price at 4.0758.
  - Exit was at **4.027, below the entry price**, through `LivePositionProtectionService.java:210-216` (lock triggered, then hard floor 4.0350165 breached).
- Per `PositionExitPolicy.evaluateProfitLockBreach`, a lock breach exits immediately unless one of two hold conditions is met:
  - 1h is bullish and 5m is supportive, or
  - 1h is WATCH, 5m is supportive and 1m is not bearish.
- 1h was NEUTRAL at entry. If it was still NEUTRAL, the first price at or below 4.0758 should have produced `PROFIT_LOCK_EXIT` at about 4.07.
- Three explanations are possible:
  - **(a)** The price gapped from ≥4.0758 straight to ≤4.035 between price events.
  - **(b)** Earlier lock breaches were held (1h had changed to WATCH and 5m was supportive).
  - **(c)** The lock was not executable while it was rebasing to the extended TP.
- I cannot decide between them until I see the exact price path (I2-001) and the timeframe signals (I2-002).
- Production does not write PROFIT_LOCK_HOLD to `position_management_event`; only Replay writes it (ShadowProductionReplayService:488/618). The application logs (`Live PROFIT_LOCK protected hold`) may be needed.
- Near-TP was `NEAR_TP_ARMED` with best price 4.101 and the harvest was never used. It ranks last in the exit priority, so this is consistent with the design.
