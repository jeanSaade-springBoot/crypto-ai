## FINDINGS

### F-22: trade 143's loss below the floor was a sub-second liquidity sweep that no kline-driven protection could have stopped above the floor (EXTERNAL RESEARCH + PROJECT EVIDENCE; strengthens F-17)

EGLDUSDT `aggTrades` from Binance (epoch ms converted to UTC, KSA = UTC+3):
- **Last trade before the drop:** 19:43:26.041 UTC (22:43:26.041 KSA) at 4.100.
- **No trades at all** until 19:43:34.341 UTC. The 8-second gap in `market_price_event` (I3-005) was genuine market inactivity, not lost messages. Other symbols kept streaming in that window (BTC 3 events, ICP 5).
- **The sweep:** 19:43:34.816 UTC, all within one millisecond, from 4.094 down to 4.073. This crossed the lock level of 4.0758.
- **Below the floor** (4.0350): 4.026 at 34.993. The time from lock to floor was **177 ms**.
- **Next kline push:** E = 19:43:35.021 UTC, close 4.027. The wallet SELL ran at 22:43:35.046 KSA, 25 ms after that push.

**AGENT INFERENCE:**
- Both levels were crossed between two consecutive kline pushes.
- Even a trade-level feed with about 150 ms of network delay would have exited near 4.03.
- Only a resting exchange-side stop order could have protected the lock level, and that is out of scope for paper trading.
- **There is no protection defect.** The loss comes from the target extensions (F-12) meeting a liquidity air-pocket.

### F-23: correction of my own F-19. The exits of 137 and 158 were triggered by price ticks; the JVM clock runs behind Binance (PROJECT EVIDENCE + EXTERNAL RESEARCH + AGENT INFERENCE)

How the timestamps are produced:
- `observed_at` is Binance event time `E` (`BinanceKlineService.java:94`).
- `executed_at` is the uncorrected JVM `Instant.now()` (`WalletAutoExecutionService.java:178/263/357/451/548/661`).
- The live exit runs **only** inside `LivePositionProtectionService.onPrice`, which is called for each 1m kline event (`BinanceKlineService.java:108`).

Trade 137:
- EGLD trades occurred at 18:00:00.029 UTC and **18:00:07.638 UTC** (3.987).
- The next push after the exit signals had committed (21:00:04.327 KSA) was E = 18:00:09.033 UTC (21:00:09.033 KSA) at 3.987.
- The SELL is stamped 21:00:08.935 KSA, which is **−98 ms** relative to that push. Trade 158 shows the same pattern at **−102 ms**.

Across the five tick-driven exits, the gap from tick to execution ranges from −102 to +57 ms. That is only consistent with the JVM clock trailing Binance by about 0.1–0.2 s, plus network delay.

**Corrections:**
- My iteration-3 statement "something other than a price tick triggered this exit" is **withdrawn**. Both exits were triggered by ticks, and the P&L is unaffected.
- **New audit gap:** audit times mix the uncorrected JVM clock (`executed_at`, `generated_at`, `closed_at`) with exchange time (`observed_at`). Freshness checks do use the corrected clock (`MarketPriceEventService:39`). Proof requested in I4-003.

### F-24: the exit authorities are asymmetric, and Replay probably differs in exit timing (PROJECT EVIDENCE + AGENT INFERENCE)

1. **The live normal exit has no time cap.** It reads the newest 1m/5m/1h by `generatedAt` with no cap and no freshness check (`LPPS:95-97`). BUY and `validateSell` instead use the at-or-before cap on the 1m's `generatedAt` (`TEVS:367-378`).
   - At a 5m boundary, the 1m SELL of 137, 158 and 182 saw the superseded 5m in `validateSell`.
   - The **mechanical** path then closed on the next tick using the new 5m.
2. **The live normal exit waits for a trade.** It is only evaluated on a tick, never when a signal is committed:
   - 137: exit 4.6 s after the signals committed;
   - 172: 5m NEUTRAL committed at 07:15:05.866 and the exit ran at 07:15:43.492, a **37.6 s** wait. SHIB's 07:14 candle had 0 trades (I3-001).
3. **Replay evaluates `evaluateNormalExit` when the signal arrives** (`ShadowProductionReplayService:629-633`). So Replay can exit earlier, at the signal price rather than the next tick.

**Impact on these 6 trades:** none on P&L (137: 3.988 vs 3.987; 172: 5.48 vs 5.48).

**Status:** this is a parity and observability risk on illiquid symbols, not a cause of these losses.
