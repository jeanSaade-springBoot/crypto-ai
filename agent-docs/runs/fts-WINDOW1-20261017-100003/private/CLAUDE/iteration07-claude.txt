### F-44: F-42 / M2 is reframed: the floor-stop class is the *less* bad class, and fees are the real constraint (PROJECT EVIDENCE: I6-003)
Floor-stop and ATR-stop classes compared:

| Class | n | Loss rate | Stopped | Gross P&L | Gross per trade | Estimated fee at 0.2% | Estimated net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FLOOR_STOP | 93 | 45% | 23 (25%) | −0.793 | −0.0085 | 4.638 | **−5.43** |
| ATR_STOP | 100 | 59% | 52 (52%) | −3.378 | −0.0338 | 7.752 | **−11.13** |

These totals are my arithmetic over the I6-003 rows. They add up to 193 trades.

- **Iteration 6 said these positions "almost never reach the stop". That was wrong:** 25% of floor-stop trades were stopped out. It only held for the take-profit < 0.40% subset in I5-004.
- **What survives (AGENT INFERENCE from the I6-003 ratios):** floor-stop winners average about **0.20–0.22% gross** of position value (`PROFIT_LOCK` 0.222%, `PROFIT_LOCK_EXIT` 0.205%). That is about the same as a 0.15–0.20% round trip, so even the winners are close to zero after fees. ATR-stop winners make 0.38–1.13%.
- **Also wrong in iteration 6:** "`fee_usdt = 0` on every row" was too broad. `paper_position` has **no fee column** (I6-003 schema). `wallet_trade.fee_usdt` is 0 for the six trades' fills (I1-006); whether that holds system-wide is unverified (I7-001).
